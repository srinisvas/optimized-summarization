**Executive Summary**

This paper talks about how to keep data private in smart factories (industrial IoT) while still allowing them to work efficiently. The main idea is that traditional privacy methods don't work well when lots of data is shared quickly. The authors propose a new system called Quantum-resistant Differential Privacy (QRDP) that uses special encryption and noise to protect data while still allowing analysis. They tested this system on simulated traffic data and found it works faster than older methods. The main point is that QRDP balances security and usefulness.

The paper also discusses privacy issues in different types of smart factory architectures, like cloud-based and edge-based. It reviews existing privacy solutions for these architectures and compares their privacy performance and computational complexity. The authors found that while privacy protection is possible, there are still challenges, especially with energy constraints and integrating new technologies like SDN and blockchain.

The paper concludes by highlighting open research issues, such as how to design energy-efficient privacy protection algorithms for edge devices and how to integrate new technologies like 5G into smart factories while maintaining privacy. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of privacy issues in smart factories and potential solutions.
