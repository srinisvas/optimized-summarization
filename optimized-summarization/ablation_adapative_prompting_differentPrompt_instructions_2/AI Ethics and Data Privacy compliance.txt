**Executive Summary**

This paper discusses the ethical and privacy issues related to artificial intelligence (AI) and data privacy. It highlights the importance of protecting human rights in the context of AI development and its applications. The paper compares the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) with AI, pointing out significant differences in how they handle data privacy and human rights.

The GDPR is a set of rules that applies to all organizations collecting and retaining personal data in the European Union. It emphasizes transparency, data minimization, accuracy, integrity, confidentiality, responsibility, and the right to lodge a complaint. In contrast, AI is not focused on protecting individual human rights and often lacks mechanisms for individual protection actions.

The paper also discusses the ethical challenges of AI, such as the potential for AI systems to infringe on human rights and the lack of transparency in AI algorithms. It suggests that AI developers have an ethical obligation to make their algorithms transparent and to explain their reasoning, especially when they can identify ethical issues in use.

The paper concludes that while AI has the potential to bring significant benefits, it also poses significant ethical and privacy challenges. It calls for a legislative framework that can adapt to AI applications and emphasizes the need for cooperation and collaboration among various stakeholders, including companies, universities, and research institutions, to ensure respect for private human rights in AI applications.

Overall, the paper argues that GDPR provides a solid foundation for data privacy, but it is not sufficient on its own. A more comprehensive approach that includes ethical considerations and transparency in AI development is necessary to protect human rights and ensure responsible AI use.
