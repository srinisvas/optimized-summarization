**Executive Summary**

This research paper discusses the challenges of privacy policies in mobile health-Cloud environments. It highlights the need for formal languages to express privacy preferences and negotiate conflicting policies. The authors propose an approach that integrates the patient as a key negotiator, using a Security Policy Negotiation Framework. They present a case study of three conflicting privacy policies and demonstrate how their approach can resolve these conflicts by negotiating privacy preferences and policies.

Their solution is based on a framework that allows for negotiation between different parties, including patients, healthcare providers, and cloud providers. They use a privacy policy language called P2U to express patients' preferences and a negotiation process that considers the patient's privacy group and the purpose of data usage. The negotiation process involves stages such as conflict detection, group determination, and contract establishment, with the bargaining stage playing a crucial role in reaching an agreement.

The paper emphasizes the importance of balancing security and usability in cloud-based mobile health systems, where conflicts between privacy policies can arise due to the diversity of actors involved. The authors propose a patient-centric approach that not only formalizes privacy preferences but also facilitates negotiation to resolve conflicts, ensuring that patients have control over their data while maintaining privacy. Future work includes developing each stage of the approach, particularly the bargaining stage, and exploring the use of this framework in real-world scenarios.
