This paper addresses the privacy risks faced by customers in open cloud environments, where malicious service providers might record service data and deduce private information. While noise obfuscation—injecting noise service requests to confuse providers—is an effective protection method, existing strategies (such as RNGS, HPNGS, and TPNGS) typically operate under worst-case assumptions, leading to the injection of excessive noise data and incurring unnecessary costs for customers in the pay-as-you-go model.

To overcome this efficiency gap, the authors propose a novel **Privacy-leakage-Tolerance based Noise Enhancing Strategy (PTNES)**.

### Key Contributions and Mechanism:

1.  **Incorporating Customer Tolerance:** PTNES integrates the **Customer-defined Privacy-Leakage-Tolerance ($\text{PLT}_C$)**—a boundary representing the maximum acceptable probability of privacy leakage defined by the user.
2.  **Optimizing Noise Set Creation:** The strategy introduces the **Privacy-leakage-Tolerance based Noise Generation Set Creation Model (PTNCM)**. This model analyzes the **Real Privacy Leakage Risk ($\text{PLR}_R$)** under existing noise obfuscation and calculates the minimum required number of noise data items ($y_{min}$) necessary to ensure $\text{PLR}_R$ is equal to or less than $\text{PLT}_C$.
3.  **Efficiency Enhancement:** By creating this minimal, cost-evaluated noise generation set, PTNES effectively guides and enhances existing noise generation strategies, enabling them to reduce the volume of injected noise data (referred to as "Noise Cost"). This results in a lower operational cost for the customer with a controlled and reasonable level of privacy protection effectiveness.

### Results:

Simulation evaluations demonstrate that PTNES significantly improves the efficiency of privacy protection on existing noise obfuscation methods. When applying PTNES to typical strategies, the Noise Cost (the percentage of noise service requests) is substantially reduced. For example, PTNES reduced Noise Cost from approximately 0.95 to 0.6 when enhancing the Random Noise Generation Strategy (RNGS), and from 0.28 to 0.02 when enhancing the Historical Probability based Noise Generation Strategy (HPNGS), demonstrating considerable cost savings linked directly to the customer's defined risk tolerance.

In conclusion, PTNES provides a critical enhancement to noise obfuscation by linking privacy protection requirements directly to operational cost efficiency, making privacy protection more practical and economical for cloud customers.
