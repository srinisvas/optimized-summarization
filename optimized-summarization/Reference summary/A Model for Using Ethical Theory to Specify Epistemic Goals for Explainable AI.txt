This paper addresses the normative requirements for Explainable AI (XAI) through the lens of moral accountability, arguing that current XAI methods are insufficient to address the "responsibility gap"—the difficulty in assigning blame for morally problematic AI behaviors (such as those leading to human harm).

### Core Argument and Framework

The central concern is that highly complex, deep-learning AIs are "blackboxes," making their inner workings inscrutable. While we can assess an AI's output (its behavior), we cannot assess its potential moral accountability, which requires epistemic access to its internal content and structure (its "motivations-in-acting").

To provide the necessary normative guidance, the author applies **Nomy Arpaly's account of moral-worthiness** (praise- and blame-worthiness). This ethical framework posits that an agent is morally accountable only if they demonstrate **"reasons responsiveness"**—the capacity to perceive morally relevant reasons and be motivated by those reasons when settling on an action.

### Key Contributions and Epistemic Goals for XAI

The application of Arpaly's theory yields three specific, practical constraints that XAI tools must satisfy to close the responsibility gap and correctly assess an AI's moral status and culpability:

1.  **Perceptual Capacity:** XAI must reveal whether the AI has an input system capable of obtaining and processing information about the world, specifically concerning **morally relevant facts**.
2.  **Granularity of Reasons:** XAI must be fine-grained enough to identify the **precise features of the action** the AI used to select a behavior, confirming what specific reasons (moral or otherwise) drove its action.
3.  **Motivational System:** XAI must confirm whether the AI is capable of using these morally relevant reasons **as reasons for acting** (i.e., whether it possesses a functional motivational system).

### Conclusion

The paper concludes that failure by developers to accommodate these normative requirements will cause humanity to miss the crucial moment when sophisticated AI systems acquire the level of moral status required to be held accountable for their behavior. These epistemic goals serve as essential prerequisites for any XAI tool designed to facilitate moral judgment and ensure appropriate accountability for future advanced AI.
