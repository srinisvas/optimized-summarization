This paper addresses the critical challenge of protecting sensitive location data within the emerging paradigm of edge computing, which is necessary to overcome the limitations of centralized cloud computing in the Internet of Everything (IoE). Due to the risk posed by untruthful third parties (honest-but-curious entities like edge data centers), the paper rejects Centralized Differential Privacy (CDP) and focuses on Local Differential Privacy (LDP), which allows users to perturb their data locally before transmission.

**Proposed Method:**

The paper proposes a novel location data collection method that integrates spatial division with LDP to enhance privacy and data availability:

1.  **Road Network Division:** A Voronoi diagram, constructed using the Delaunay method, is used to divide the road network space into secure Voronoi grids. These grids define security areas, mapping edge nodes to the grid generators (road intersections).
2.  **LDP Disturbance:** Within each Voronoi grid, a random disturbance mechanism (based on Random Response) is utilized. Users generate their real location and several false locations within the grid boundary, then use an $\epsilon$-LDP mechanism to randomly report one location, thereby protecting the accurate position from edge nodes and data collectors.

**Performance Evaluation and Key Findings:**

The proposed Voronoi diagram-based LDP mechanism was evaluated using the Gowalla dataset and compared against classical privacy methods: CDP with Laplace noise, CDP with Gaussian white noise, and k-anonymity, using Quality-of-Service Loss (Qloss) and Privacy-Preserving Level (PL) as metrics.

*   **Higher Data Availability:** The proposed LDP mechanism consistently achieved the **least Qloss** (highest data availability) across all experiments compared to the competing methods.
*   **Insensitivity to Privacy Level:** Unlike the comparison mechanisms—where Qloss significantly increased as the desired Privacy Level (PL) increased—the Qloss of the proposed LDP mechanism remained **virtually unchanged** with increasing PL, demonstrating robust performance regardless of high privacy requirements.
*   **Scalability:** With an increase in the number of users, the LDP mechanism showed the minimal increase in Qloss, indicating better algorithm performance and scalability.

**Conclusion:**

The proposed location data collection method satisfies LDP properties and effectively protects user location privacy in the edge computing environment. The combination of Voronoi spatial division and random disturbance provides comprehensive protection against untruthful entities while ensuring higher data availability than existing classical privacy-preserving techniques. Future work will focus on optimizing resource consumption (as generating multiple fake locations is resource-intensive) and exploring the convergence of edge computing, LDP, and blockchain technology.
