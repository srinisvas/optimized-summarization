This paper addresses the growing societal anxiety surrounding the rapid, un-discussed deployment of new Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, such as ChatGPT and Google Bard. These public fears and unfounded rumors threaten the advancement and adoption of beneficial AI-integrated technologies, particularly in the Internet of Things (IoT) sector.

To mitigate this risk and ensure public support, the article proposes that all AI and Autonomous and Intelligent Systems (A/IS) projects, including those involving IoT, must engage in collective social discussion guided by the **General Principles of the IEEE Ethically Aligned Design (EAD) guide.**

The EAD framework mandates a human-centered approach, prioritizing **human well-being** over purely technical objectives. This framework is built upon three pillars (Universal Human Values, Political Self-Determination and Data Agency, and Technical Dependability) and enforces eight General Principles for development and implementation, including:

1.  **Human Rights** (respecting international law).
2.  **Well-being** (making it the primary success criterion).
3.  **Data Agency** (ensuring individuals control their identity and data).
4.  **Transparency** (making AI decisions discoverable).
5.  **Accountability** (clarifying liability and obligations).

By embedding these ethical standards, documenting development processes, and consulting with affected societal groups, developers can establish trust in AI and IoT technologies, securing the necessary social license to operate and innovate responsibly.
