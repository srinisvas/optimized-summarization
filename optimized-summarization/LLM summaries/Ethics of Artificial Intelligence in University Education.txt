**Executive Summary**
Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative technology with the potential to revolutionize various sectors, including education. This paper conducts a comprehensive literature search across academic databases, including PubMed, Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, and ERIC, to identify and synthesize key ethical dimensions surrounding the utilization of AI in higher education, focusing on scholarly works that explore the integration of AI tools, students’ perceptions, and the inclusion of ethics in AI education. The retrieved literature is screened and examined to identify connections, similarities, and differences in perspectives, highlighting research findings, theoretical frameworks, and ethical principles discussed in the context of academic integrity and AI-based learning models.

The ethical considerations identified revolve around several key themes: academic integrity, specifically problems associated with misuse such as cheating; misleading and low-quality outputs generated by artificial intelligence tools; the risks associated with education automation; and pressing concerns related to data privacy and the potential negative impacts on fundamental human rights. The necessity for transparency and scientific integrity when utilizing tools like ChatGPT is strongly emphasized across the literature. Furthermore, studies conducted in university settings reveal that students from technology and science-focused schools display a higher degree of concern about data privacy, indicating a deeper understanding of potential privacy implications, while students from the arts, humanities, and social sciences show slightly lower levels of concern regarding AI’s implementation.

To support the responsible and effective implementation of AI in university education, the research highlights the significance of establishing comprehensive strategies. These include curriculum development, the establishment of technology development labs, specialized training programs, and clear university regulations aimed at improving learning quality through AI-based models. Ultimately, the literature promotes the integration of artificial intelligence into teaching practices while ensuring that educators possess not only necessary technical skills but also the requisite pedagogical and ethical knowledge required for careful consideration and ethical oversight in the deployment of these technologies.
