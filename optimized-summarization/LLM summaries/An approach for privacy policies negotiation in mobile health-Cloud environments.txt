Executive Summary

This paper addresses the critical challenge of resolving conflicting privacy policies within mobile health (mHealth) and Cloud computing environments, where the increased adoption of Cloud-based mobile services multiplies privacy threats and necessitates maximizing patient control over sensitive data. Given the diversity of actors involved in patient care, conflicts among policies are frequent, requiring the development of formal languages, such as the Purpose-to-Use (P2U) language, to enable patients to express and control the disclosure of their privacy preferences. To manage these complex security risks, the research introduces a patient-centric approach based on a Security Policy Negotiation Framework that considers the patient a crucial actor in the negotiation process.

The proposed solution is distinguished by its ability to handle both the heterogeneity of privacy policies and the multiplicity of actors, including Cloud Computing providers. The framework classifies patients into specific groups based on their privacy preferences—for instance, categorizing individuals as "Pragmatic" patients who can negotiate their policies, or as part of groups like minors or those badly hurt who cannot make proper decisions and require a guardian. This classification methodology is intended to make the resolution of conflicting policies easier. Furthermore, the framework assumes that a Trusted Third Party (TTP) can recognize and understand numerous privacy policy languages, demonstrated through an example that resolves a conflict involving P2U, XACML, and S4P languages.

The negotiation process relies on several essential parameters, including the entity to whom the policy is addressed, the time period for which data can be retained, and, critically, the purpose of data usage. The application of the extended approach is illustrated using a real-world example of conflicting policies to demonstrate its utility in resolving complex disputes. The authors conclude that further development is needed for each stage of the framework, particularly the bargaining stage, which constitutes a crucial step toward fully resolving conflicting privacy preferences in the mobile health-Cloud ecosystem.
