**Executive Summary**

Generative AI technologies are rapidly advancing across sectors such as healthcare, education, and transportation, but this progress often incurs a significant "Values Debt"—ethical and operational deficits arising from insufficient ethical considerations during the development process. Traditional software engineering practices often prioritize code correctness and performance but neglect these critical ethical complexities, leading to risks such as public backlash, lawsuits, and diminished market share due to unethical systems. To mitigate this debt, this paper introduces and operationalizes the Helpful, Honest, and Harmless (HHH) framework, advocating for the integration of ethical system engineering practices throughout the generative AI development lifecycle to enhance trust and integrity.

The framework’s practical application is demonstrated through the case study of GRAISE, a Graph RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)-based chatbot designed for aviation safety, a high-stakes environment. To ensure adherence to the HHH principles, GRAISE integrates GPT-4 with a graph-structured aviation knowledge base, built using NASA's ASRS incident reports. By structuring data graphically, the Graph RAG system significantly reduces instances of hallucinations and misinformation prevalent in conventional LLMs, providing clearer causal links and enhancing both the diversity and completeness of answers. This reliance on validated data ensures the system avoids harmful or misleading advice, thereby fulfilling the harmlessness and honesty requirements, while also integrating privacy preservation measures like data anonymization.

The GRAISE experience highlights that operationalizing the HHH framework requires a cultural shift and cross-disciplinary collaboration, engaging technologists, domain experts, ethicists, and stakeholders to proactively mitigate ethical risks. Although implementing HHH principles may demand extra resources, the process yields long-term benefits in compliance and trust. Furthermore, the adaptability of Graph RAG systems enables continuous evaluation and modification, allowing the AI architecture to swiftly respond to evolving ethical standards and domain regulations. This methodology provides a blueprint for creating technically robust and socially responsible AI across various domains, ensuring the integration of domain knowledge, transparency of information, and application reliability.
