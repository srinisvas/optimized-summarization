Executive Summary
The increasing reliance on Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Autonomous and Intelligent Systems (A/IS), particularly within the Internet of Things (IoT) domain—which has significantly enhanced smart cities, pollution control, and energy saving—necessitates proactive engagement with society. The introduction of major AI tools, such as OpenAI ChatGPT, without prior public discussion has generated senseless fear and unfounded rumors in the global media, negatively affecting areas like education and professional careers. The paper argues that this societal apprehension could potentially hinder the beneficial development of AI-enabled IoT technologies, despite the common objective of these initiatives being the improvement of the population's quality of life. The core proposal is to discuss projects using AI or A/IS technologies, including IoT devices, with society to secure support and avoid the detrimental effects of false information.

To structure this engagement and guide development, the research advocates utilizing the General Principles outlined in the IEEE Ethically Aligned Design (EAD) guide, which serves as a conceptual framework based on anthropological, political, and technical considerations. These principles establish the foundational ethics and values for AI design, development, and implementation. Key mandates include Accountability, requiring AI to be developed to provide an unambiguous basis for all choices, and the protection of Human Rights, ensuring these rights are respected and recognized internationally through the creation and operation of AI. Furthermore, the concept of Well-being dictates that AI impacts on human welfare must be assessed, understood, measured, protected, and improved.

The implementation of robust AI policies must also address practical controls and preventative measures. This includes ensuring Data Agency, where AI creators must educate people on how to securely access and share data to maintain control over their identity. Furthermore, AI systems must be designed to protect natural resources and the environment, and creators must proactively protect against potential risks and prevent misuse (Awareness of Misuse). Transparency can be promoted through certification systems administered by independent agencies. By adopting the IEEE EAD process, it becomes possible to systematically identify pressing issues and corresponding recommendations related to AI design, thereby ensuring that well-functioning AI policies ultimately benefit the public interest while ensuring operators possess the necessary skills before running a system.
