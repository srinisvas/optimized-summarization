Executive Summary

This paper aims to analyze the current privacy and security requirements in Cloud Computing systems while highlighting new tools and open research topics, recognizing that security and privacy are major components directly determining a system’s trustworthiness and reliability. While essential characteristics of cloud computing, such as rapid elasticity, broad network access, and resource pooling, have been realized to a certain extent, the field remains a work in progress, particularly as threats to network and computing infrastructures continue to increase in sophistication. Multi-tenancy must be defined separately for each service model, such as Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS). To meet security functions, the necessary requirements include integrity, confidentiality, and availability, often overseen by a designated Cloud Auditor.

Information privacy is defined as the assured, proper, and consistent collection, processing, communication, use, and disposition of personal information (PI) and personally-identifiable information (PII) throughout its lifecycle. Solution systems should establish clear and mutually exclusive ownership rights over data. Compliance refers to an organization’s responsibility to operate, necessitating audit mechanisms and tools to ensure organizational practices; conversely, the lack of organizational controls over employees arbitrarily engaging in cloud computing services can be a significant source of problems. Furthermore, the cloud provider’s electronic discovery capabilities and processes must not compromise the privacy or security of one organization's data or applications while satisfying the discovery obligations of other cloud consumers, or vice versa.

Protecting data while it is actively in use is an emerging area of cryptography with few practical results available, leaving trust mechanisms as the main safeguard for now. The key to future security and privacy improvements lies in advanced tools like Homomorphic encryption, which would allow a Service Provider (SP) to perform calculations directly on encrypted data while yielding the same results as the consumer would get from unencrypted data. Continuous monitoring of information security is vital, requiring ongoing awareness of security controls, vulnerabilities, and threats to support risk management decisions. This robust data handling also mandates that data sanitization applies to backup copies made for recovery and restoration of service, as well as residual data remaining upon termination of service.
