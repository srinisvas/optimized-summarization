**Executive Summary**

Differential privacy (DP) offers rigorous protections for sensitive data in emerging paradigms such as federated machine learning and decentralized analytics, utilizing parameters $\epsilon$ and $\delta$ to bound the divergence between outputs and balance privacy with utility. A critical challenge limiting the widespread adoption of DP, however, is accurately quantifying the cumulative privacy loss accrued over long-running processes, which typically involve iterative model queries, validations, and complex multi-party computations. This paper proposes a comprehensive methodology designed to evaluate end-to-end differential privacy guarantees across such iterative Machine Learning (ML) workflows, thereby addressing the issue of tracking privacy erosion across the model lifecycle.

The core contribution is a heuristic algorithm that models total privacy loss as an accumulative random variable composited over successive operations. This algorithm rigorously tracks the moments of the privacy loss random variable using advanced composition theorems, allowing it to maintain a privacy budget that is systematically depleted per operation based on computed data sensitivity and necessary noise calibration. The framework’s implementation, which includes the flexible `composeLoss()` function, seamlessly handles sequential, parallel, and adaptive compositions, ensuring that the composition and moments accountant modules remain functional across diverse DP mechanisms. This systematic approach allows the system to precisely determine whether aggregate leakage exceeds predefined $(\epsilon, \delta)$ budgets, ensuring that if unsafe excessive leakage has occurred, the process can be halted.

Empirical validation utilizing simulated workloads demonstrates the algorithm's effectiveness in verifying differential privacy guarantees for processes involving hundreds of iterative computations. For instance, the technique successfully quantified privacy loss across 950 successive histogram queries under $(\epsilon = 1, \delta = 10^{-5})$-differential privacy while sustaining utility with an average error of only 4.5% compared to non-private histograms; furthermore, under the slightly relaxed budget of $(\epsilon = 2, \delta = 10^{-3})$, the system sustained over 2900 iterations. By tuning noise parameters, acceptable accuracy is maintained, providing a practical, measurable solution for privacy-preserving ML pipelines that minimizes utility degradation, thereby laying a rigorous foundation for developing trustworthy AI systems.
