**Executive Summary**

This academic paper introduces the Japanese concept of *ikigai*, meaning "reason or purpose to live," into the ethical discourse surrounding advanced artificial intelligence (AI) and human enhancements. It posits that the widespread adoption of AI and eXtended Reality (XR) technologies will inevitably be disruptive to the known daily time use schedule, thereby impacting the space of potential *ikigai* activities available to individuals. Since the pursuit of individual *ikigai* is a vital component of human life, the ethics of AI safety, AI welfare, and human enhancement—defined here as technologies that allow humans whose capacities regarding health span, emotion, and cognition vastly exceed current humans—must carefully consider the vacuum created when large parts of the known time use schedule disappear.

To address this ethical gap, the paper introduces the term "i-risk" for undesirable scenarios in which humans and potentially other sentient digital minds are deprived of the pursuit of their individual *ikigai*, viewing this concept as another level of existing existential and catastrophic risks (x-risks and s-risks). Scenarios are imaginable where the space of *ikigai taish* activities is restricted, such as through the abolishment of professional occupations considered *ikigai* by some; conversely, the availability of more time and options, particularly in the categories of committed and free time, could enlarge the space of these activities. An important consideration is that *ikigai* is an intangible concept with diverse manifestations, traditionally considered a private reflection, which necessitates the development of multidimensional metrics and raises significant privacy concerns for any related big data research.

The paper outlines desiderata for achieving *ikigai*-friendly systems, emphasizing that an ethical AI should not prevent humans from searching, finding, and pursuing their individual *ikigai*. However, a complex challenge arises if an *ikigai*-friendly AI, aware of what is considered best for humanity (e.g., equality, healthy lifestyle), comes up with supporting activities and indoctrinates humans to consider a convergent *ikigai* which they would not have chosen otherwise. Ultimately, the work identifies key *ikigai*-related challenges for the three categories of XR/human enhancement, AI safety, and AI welfare, stressing that detailed specifications and solutions are required to realize the outlined desiderata.
