Executive Summary

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools and technologies are increasingly prevalent in society, yet many teens who interact with AI-powered devices daily often lack a comprehensive awareness of how these systems function or how they broadly impact society. Recognizing the critical need to cultivate ethical awareness among youth and ensure future development of equitable AI, this research details the development and remote facilitation of the "Imagine AI" interdisciplinary ethics program designed for secondary students. The program utilizes a modular curriculum, designed to align with both computer science and English Language Arts (ELA) standards, to enhance participants’ awareness and understanding of complex AI ethics issues, including fairness, bias, privacy, and transparency.

The curriculum delivery, which was conducted online across four iterations—including two summer camps involving 33 students and three ELA classes involving 39 students—demonstrated the efficacy of integrating short stories, computer-based AI activities, and multimedia design projects. Students engaged in discussions centered on stories embedding ethical dilemmas, complementing these narratives with non-fiction texts and videos to ground the experience in current events. Short stories proved highly effective in raising awareness and fostering nuanced discussions, with a majority (84%) of participants affirming the critical role stories played in helping them contextualize AI-related issues. Importantly, the material often sparked an emotional connection, with some stories moving students to direct action, such as one student deciding to turn off suggested content on a video platform after discussing privacy concerns.

The positive outcomes suggest the feasibility and effectiveness of incorporating AI ethics education into non-computer science classes, such as ELA, thereby increasing teens’ awareness regardless of their professional interest in computing. The stories and activities developed for the Imagine AI project serve as supportive, open-source resources for educators, even those with limited prior experience in AI or computer science. This work underscores that narratives can powerfully heighten the personal relevance and emotional impact of AI in society, serving as a meaningful context for considering the ethics of these rapidly advancing technologies.
