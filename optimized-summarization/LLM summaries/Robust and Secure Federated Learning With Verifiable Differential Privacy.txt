**Executive Summary**

Federated Learning (FL) is highly vulnerable to both Byzantine attacks and data privacy breaches, challenges which existing defense mechanisms struggle to address simultaneously due to theoretical incompatibilities that often necessitate sacrificing either accuracy or robustness. To overcome this critical gap, this paper introduces a novel FL framework designed to maintain training accuracy while balancing privacy protection and Byzantine resilience (BR). The key architectural innovation involves utilizing secondary servers to separately handle robustness evaluation and differential privacy (DP) noise injection, thereby enabling the system to detect malicious local updates without interference before securely adding verifiable DP noise during aggregation. This separation ensures reliable model updates and leverages three core design principles: the use of secondary servers for divided responsibilities, the integration of secure multi-party computation (MPC)-based verification with Pedersen commitments, and the distribution of noise generation across multiple secondary servers to lower the individual noise burden while still meeting DP guarantees.

The technical foundation of the framework rests upon a noise-added Shamir secret sharing protocol, which incorporates $(\epsilon, \delta)$-DP noise directly into the sharing process. By utilizing cryptographic commitments and secure MPC among users, the methodology avoids the noise amplification issues commonly observed in traditional approaches, securing both the local and global update phases against adaptive threats. Pedersen’s VSS (Verifiable Secret Sharing), which features an additive homomorphic property, is integral to this process, ensuring that adversarial collusion cannot bypass BR checks or reconstruct raw updates. Under the Binomial Mechanism, the global model satisfies $(\epsilon, \delta)$-differential privacy, where the per-server noise parameter $n_b$ scales favorably, being inversely proportional to the number of secondary servers used.

Experimental validation across MNIST, FMNIST, and CIFAR-10 datasets demonstrates the superior performance of the proposed approach. The framework maintains nearly 98% of the baseline accuracy when the privacy budget $\epsilon$ is set to $\geq 0.1$, and it exhibits robust tolerance, handling up to 60% malicious users effectively. Furthermore, the system enhances accuracy by 0.57% to 4.16% compared to existing methods that attempt to combine DP and BR, showing strong resistance against various adaptive threats, including model poisoning, label flipping, and backdoor injections. The framework also exhibits linear scalability with the number of participants, concluding that this approach, which separates robustness checking from DP noise injection, offers a highly promising pathway for FL deployment in privacy-sensitive sectors such as healthcare and smart grids.
