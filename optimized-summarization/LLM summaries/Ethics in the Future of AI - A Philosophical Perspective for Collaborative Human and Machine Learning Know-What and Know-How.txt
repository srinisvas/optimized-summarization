Executive Summary

This paper addresses the fundamental need for philosophical grounding, knowledge, and skills among specialists developing Generative AI (GenAI) and pursuing Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), arguing that current ethical approaches are insufficient to manage associated logical challenges, risks, and costs. The necessity for a philosophical perspective is highlighted by the confirmed risks of AI overtaking human capabilities in narrow applications and the ethical dilemmas posed by GenAI’s dual capacity for creating and detecting academic misconduct, such as plagiarism, within scientific environments characterized by fluid data. To mitigate these issues, the research proposes a formal, humanistic meta-design framework centered on Kantian ethics, which provides a twofold opportunity for moral AI design: an *a priori* transcendental integration of ethical attitude within the AI design loop, and an *a posteriori* experimental evaluation by both human and artificial entities using established metrics and case studies.

The proposed ethical meta-design framework is built on the pragmatic vision that AI systems possess the potential for non-deterministic, continuous adaptation and learning, making the 'use time' a critical phase where ethical constraints must evolve beyond initial design. This approach utilizes entropy metrics, such as Knowledge Entropy, which assists in assessing scientific discoveries by connecting the philosophical interplay of epistemology with the rating of physical reality through probabilities. The evolution of the system involves the dynamic adaptation of AI know-what and know-how based on interaction patterns, the trustful evolution of knowledge representations, and the emergence of new capabilities through sustained human-AI collaboration and continuously evaluated refinement of understanding. By acknowledging and creating this fluid bridge between design and operational phases, the framework ensures that human designers, developers, evaluators, and users become active co-participants in the system’s evolution, advocating for a DNA-RNA roles analogy for hardware-AI integration to replace a potential "master-slave" relationship.

Future theoretical and practical contributions are scheduled to design experimental evidence gathering, focusing on metrics and detailed implementation statistics, particularly concerning Knowledge and Intelligence Entropies to support the theoretical arguments presented. These case studies will allow for the initial practical implementation of the proposed inclusive Framework for Ethical AI Meta-Design, which mandates co-creative checks and explicit, explainable interactive feedback between the human and machine collaborators, preferably using open-source GenAI versions. Experimental applications will concentrate on co-creative challenges connected to ethics, aesthetics, and logic, targeting domains such as arts, scientific authoring, and education, which will further detail the proposed methodologies and framework.
