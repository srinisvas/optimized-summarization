Executive Summary

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has delivered remarkable advancements to the cultural heritage sector, offering unprecedented capacities to analyze huge amounts of historical data and enabling researchers to uncover precious patterns, connections, and insights that would otherwise remain elusive. AI technologies also significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of tasks such as cataloging and organizing extensive cultural collections, streamlining heritage management for future generations. Despite these benefits, the integration of AI brings forth intricate ethical questions spanning issues of authenticity, subjectivity, and interpretation biases of AI-generated content up to legal concerns related to authorship, issues which the paper identifies as largely undefined and unaddressed in current scholarship. This work aims to pave the way to fill this gap by analyzing AI opportunities and matching them against relevant, agreed-upon AI ethics principles to highlight specific risks requiring consideration for trustworthy development and deployment.

The analysis highlights several significant ethical risks that must be considered in the cultural heritage context, including the potential loss of authenticity of artworks, the attribution of responsibility for algorithm-based decisions, and the risk of discrimination or marginalization against minorities. Specific challenges arise from the use of Generative AI, which, by making it possible to create realistic art products, raises concerns about the role of the artist and authorship, and the possibility of aesthetic standardization resulting from systems that prioritize popular contents, potentially diminishing the provocative and critical power of the arts. Moreover, attempts to assimilate indigenous knowledge into a "Western" worldview through the interpretation of cultural heritage metadata risk undermining the veracity of local cultures, while replacing physical contact with virtual experiences could jeopardize the fulfillment of the educational goals of cultural heritage through communicative and relational impoverishment.

In response to these complex challenges, this work identifies the necessity of human agency and oversight and calls for institutions, the scientific community, and experts in the field to take action by establishing specific criteria and standards that move beyond the generalist approach of existing guidelines. The objectives include identifying areas of further work and key players—including art historians, archaeologists, computer scientists, and jurists—to facilitate next steps for the ethics and governance of AI in cultural heritage. Such multidisciplinary and multi-stakeholder efforts are deemed essential to harness AI’s benefits and prevent its risks, promoting an informed use of these technologies that facilitates a relational, mutually flourishing approach between AI agency and human agency and creativity.
