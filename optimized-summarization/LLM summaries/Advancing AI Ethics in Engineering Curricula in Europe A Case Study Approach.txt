Executive Summary

This paper presents the structure and findings of the Erasmus+ Ethical Engineer project, which aims to enhance AI education by integrating essential ethical, social, and legal aspects into engineering training to promote truthful AI development within Europe. The necessity for this work is underscored by the observation that while technical proficiency remains crucial, the profound societal impact of engineering decisions demands a heightened awareness of ethical responsibilities. AI systems, with their capacity for autonomous decision-making and data-driven insights, often operate in opaque ways, making it challenging to trace accountability or foresee unintended consequences. To address these ethical concerns, the project developed a series of diverse case studies and a systematic methodology for their creation, recognizing the explosive growth witnessed in the data science discipline over the last decade.

The developed case studies cover a wide variety of ethical dilemmas spanning different areas of real life. Examples include examining AI in healthcare, such as the use of fall detection systems for vulnerable populations and the moral issues arising from balancing patient benefits with safety, informed consent, and regulatory adherence, specifically noting off-label device use like first-generation ventricular assist devices which lacked FDA approval. Other studies explore the ethical implications of facial recognition in airport boarding, the potential job displacement and authorship issues arising from generative AI in marketing processes, and the ecological impact of generative AI, referencing studies on energy and water consumption. Furthermore, the cases address pervasive issues like bias in data science, selection bias when using AI to assess historic photos, and the ethical considerations surrounding self-driving taxis and automated witness statement generation.

The primary contribution of this research is the development of a structured template designed for creating AI ethics case studies, which has been tested and refined based on feedback from smaller groups of teachers and students. This template describes the scenario, highlights key ethical concerns, and provides necessary technical and situational background information for students to understand the case, including the specific AI approach used. Characters and roles are introduced to personalize these ethical dilemmas, with the aim of fostering nuanced discussion rather than soliciting "right" versus "wrong" opinions. The recommended basic activities for engaging students with the material include group discussions, role-playing, debates, reflective writing, and discussions with guest speakers, industry experts, and/or ethicists.
