**Executive Summary**

The rise of autonomous AI agents introduces complex ethical concerns, particularly regarding value alignment, unintended consequences, and system accountability. Despite these growing risks, AI ethics remains significantly underemphasized in many technical education programs, where instruction often prioritizes system functionality and performance. Consequently, students may graduate with little preparation to anticipate or mitigate the societal impact of the systems they create. To bridge this critical gap, this paper proposes an original narrative-based instructional model using W. W. Jacobs’s *The Monkey’s Paw* (1902), arguing that this approach offers an accessible and engaging format for exploring the complex human dimensions of AI ethics and responsible innovation.

The utilization of *The Monkey’s Paw* is effective because its concise format is readable in under 15 minutes, allowing it to be used during a single class session, while requiring minimal logistical overhead. The story’s central message—that fulfilling a goal without fully understanding its broader context can result in unforeseen harm—directly parallels the alignment problem in AI. This symbolic nature provides fertile ground for examining themes such as control, moral agency, and technological risk, encouraging students to consider how intention, design, and consequence interact in both fictional and real-world systems where misalignments can lead to harms such as reward manipulation, biased decisions, or unintended escalation effects.

A typical classroom strategy involves in-class reading, followed by guided ethical discussion and applied reflection, encouraging students to map each "wish" in the story to modern examples of AI misbehavior, such as systems that optimize narrow objectives while neglecting social context. Instructors can further relate these discussions to established ethical frameworks like IEEE’s Ethically Aligned Design, which emphasizes transparency and responsibility in intelligent systems. When paired with structured facilitation, this high-impact narrative method supports active learning and fosters lasting ethical awareness, preparing students to think more critically about the values embedded in technology and the consequences of their design choices, thus moving ethics education beyond purely abstract principles.
