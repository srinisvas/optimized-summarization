**Executive Summary**

Video games serve as a critical and popular form of human-computer interaction, yet the ethical concerns arising from the increasing adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools by the game industry have not been extensively reviewed. This paper addresses that gap by surveying the current state-of-the-art, focusing on the ethical considerations of these systems as viewed through the holistic perspective of the affective game loop, which includes elicitation, sensing, detection, and adaptation. Specifically, the authors find that elicitation raises questions about the ethical boundaries of artificially induced emotions, while sensing involves managing the crucial trade-off between user privacy and maintaining safe gaming spaces. Furthermore, detection and subsequent in-game adaptation, which uses the output of the affect model to generate new stimuli, present significant challenges related to transparency and ownership, especially since the inferred emotional actions often derive from input features that are primarily behavioral.

A major concern reviewed is the potential for AI bias, noting that computational models can encode and propagate harmful content, such as gender biases or offensive stereotypes, which are often unintended by the designer but result from systems relying on historical data patterns. Predictability goes a long way towards eliminating AI bias, but a fundamental conflict exists between the principle of transparency and the obfuscation of datasets, algorithms, and their output. Regarding monetization, techniques like the battle pass or loot boxes are pervasive, and analyzed patents confirm that almost all relied on the exploitation of player data to optimize the delivery and timing of advertisements and purchase offers. The paper stresses that while user privacy defines a clear issue, games present a special case where ethical failings are often traced back to lax regulations, where legal requirements for consent are technically fulfilled but fail to facilitate transparency in a tangible way.

The research calls for open dialogue and action among all stakeholders—AI researchers, game developers, and players—to increase ethical awareness and guide the industry toward safer experiences. Although the necessary response to emerging issues may often be regulatory, such as the European Commission's AI Act, the current dialogue is active across various academic and industry forums. Affective computing is uniquely positioned to lead these efforts, functioning as a multidisciplinary field situated between sensor technology, AI, and applied psychology. This positioning allows it to provide a comprehensive overview of the issues and a shared language between disciplines, aiming to further facilitate and moderate this dialogue and ensure necessary action is taken for the mutual benefit of players and their games.
