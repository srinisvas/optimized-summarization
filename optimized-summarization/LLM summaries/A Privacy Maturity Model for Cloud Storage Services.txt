Executive Summary

This paper presents a Privacy Maturity Model designed for Cloud Storage Services offered by Cloud Computing Providers, aiming to address current barriers and establish an effective level of privacy for sensitive data within these environments. A core challenge identified is the lack of a formal agreement establishing a standard privacy level between the service provider and the client, which is complicated by the fact that the concept of privacy varies greatly and is intrinsic to a particular country or jurisdiction’s cultures. Significant threats raised by the CSA include Data Loss or Leakage, which increases in the cloud computing environment due to the architectural and operational characteristics of the cloud, becoming critically dangerous when providers lack a proper policy to control physical access to internal resources, potentially violating client privacy.

To solve the problems inherent in this context, the research defines hypotheses and objectives based on metrics, proposing to establish privacy rights related to the collection, use, retention, and disclosure of personal information, following guidelines such as the Generally Accepted Privacy Principles (GAPP). The proposal aims to deepen existing results regarding privacy compliances and makes use of concepts defined in the BSIMM-CP model, which focuses on normative Compliance Policies and governance to identify absences or flaws in specifications relating to Personally Identifiable Information (PII). This management of processes for handling personal data must be performed across the item’s entire life cycle, from conception until the end of its availability, ensuring necessary protections similar to those required by standards such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) for health-related data.

The methodology defined to achieve these objectives is structured into four working phases, beginning with macro-activities such as Gathering Data, Formulation of the Problem, and Construction of Hypotheses and research. The study aims to present an architectural model planned as a prototype, which is set in stages for research and implementation, and seeks to resolve complex questions regarding how to identify data that is considerably sensitive, noting that many existing conformities do not meet all the necessary fields. The final phase involves conducting a detailed evaluation of the proposed technique through defined experiments, thereby establishing an achievable maturity level for cloud storage services.
