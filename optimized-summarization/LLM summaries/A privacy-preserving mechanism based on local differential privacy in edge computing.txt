**Executive Summary**

The efficient processing of big data requires the effective combination of edge and cloud computing, which subsequently intensifies the importance of establishing robust privacy-preserving mechanisms, particularly given that location data often contains a large amount of sensitive information. Recognizing the limitations of Centralized Differential Privacy (CDP)—which relies on the assumption of truthful third-party data collectors—this research focuses on Local Differential Privacy (LDP), an emerging model that processes data privacy directly on the side of users, thereby breaking the central trust requirement. Previous methods often proved unsuitable for lightweight edge devices with limited resources and failed to quantify the privacy level against background knowledge attacks. To address these deficiencies, the paper proposes a novel location data collection method that satisfies LDP in edge computing environments.

The proposed location privacy-preserving mechanism utilizes a Voronoi diagram to divide the road network space, thereby determining the Voronoi grid region associated with the edge nodes. The deployment begins with the cloud server sending the Voronoi diagram division of a map to the clients, followed by edge nodes grouping users and feeding grouping information back to the clients. The process involves connecting circumcircle centers of adjacent triangles to form a polygon network, and the location data within each resulting Voronoi grid is subsequently disturbed by a random disturbance mechanism. This privacy algorithm is designed to satisfy $\epsilon$-LDP, where the added noise is demonstrated to be inversely proportional to the privacy parameter $\epsilon$.

The performance of the Voronoi diagram-based LDP mechanism was evaluated against three classical mechanisms using comparison experiments conducted on the MATLAB 2016a platform. The results verify that the proposed mechanism successfully meets users’ privacy needs in edge computing while simultaneously achieving higher data availability compared to the existing alternatives. However, the mechanism’s requirement that each user generates multiple fake locations to protect their real location may lead to increased resource consumption. Future research will concentrate on refining the privacy-preserving model to align better with the inherent self-characteristics of edge computing and on exploring the convergence of edge computing and blockchain—identified as the trend of the IoT—to develop new and more effective privacy-preserving solutions.
