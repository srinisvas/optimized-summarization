Executive Summary

Cloud computing environments, while offering cost savings and utility, present significant privacy challenges, as the openness allows potentially malicious service providers to record customer data and collectively deduce private information without authorization. Noise obfuscation is a recognized approach to mitigate this risk, effectively generating and injecting noise service requests ($Q_N$) into real customer requests ($Q_R$) to create an obfuscated set ($Q_S$), thereby making it difficult for providers to distinguish real ones due to similar occurrence probabilities. However, existing typical noise generation strategies fail to account for the customer-defined privacy-leakage-tolerance, a specific boundary of acceptable privacy leakage possibility that could optimize the noise obfuscation process, allowing customers to obtain lower noise costs in the pay-as-you-go cloud model while maintaining effective protection.

To address this critical gap, this research proposes a novel Privacy-leakage-Tolerance based Noise Enhancing Strategy (PTNES) for privacy protection in cloud computing. The PTNES methodology first analyzes the privacy-leakage-tolerance for cloud customers regarding noise generation and subsequently presents the creation of a specialized Noise Generation Set, supported by models like the Privacy-leakage-Tolerance based Noise Injection Model (PTNIM). This set guides and manages noise generation processes, allowing for an efficient decrease in the 'Noise Cost,' which is the term used to denote the cost of noise service requests and express the overall efficiency of privacy protection on noise obfuscation. This strategy effectively 'enhances' noise obfuscation by factoring in this specific, customer-defined boundary.

The simulation evaluation demonstrated that the PTNES strategy significantly decreases the required noise cost compared to other noise generation strategies, substantially improving the efficiency of privacy protection on noise obfuscation in cloud environments. The primary contribution is a mechanism that gives cloud customers specific choices on noise obfuscation processes, aligning the cost of privacy protection with a reasonable and tolerable risk boundary. Future research will build upon PTNES by investigating how to protect customer privacy in scenarios where unethical or malicious service providers may collaborate together to deduce customer data.
