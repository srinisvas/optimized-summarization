Executive Summary

Codes of Ethics function as broad guidelines for professional engineers, upholding the highest standards of integrity and practice by focusing on protecting the public interest, ensuring professional competence, managing conflicts of interest, and maintaining social responsibility. Although different professional engineering societies across various continents—including Africa, Asia, Australia, Europe, and Latin America—employ distinct codes, they share fundamental teachings regarding ethical conduct and decision-making. This research investigated the viability of a unified global code by examining the compatibility of the IEEE Code of Ethics against the codes utilized by 32 international professional engineering societies.

The examination revealed that only four countries had adopted the IEEE Code of Ethics in its entirety, while the remaining 28 countries exhibited variations. These variations involved adopting some IEEE articles, ignoring others, and introducing entirely new articles unique to the local professional landscape. Analysis of these international articles strongly suggests that socio-political and cultural differences are the main causes of such divergences, with some articles, like those prohibiting unfair competition with another engineer through criticism or taking advantage of a privileged position, being clearly culture- based and appearing in international codes but not the IEEE standard.

Ultimately, while the study successfully identified specific articles present in international codes that are absent from the IEEE standard, the findings demonstrate that a global professional code of ethics is both conceivable and achievable. Ethical codes are crucial for professional accountability, allowing the public to understand what is expected of engineers, especially since engineering activities can give rise to adverse consequences. The primary conclusion is that a global code of ethics acceptable to all engineering societies is possible and can be produced with minimal conscientious effort, provided that adequate accommodations are made for identified cultural differences.
