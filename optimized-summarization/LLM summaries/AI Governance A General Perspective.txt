**Executive Summary**

This paper addresses the urgent and critical topic of Artificial Intelligence (AI) governance, necessitated by the widespread use of AI across vital fields such as healthcare, transportation, research, and the judicial system. While AI offers enormous potential, its multiple applications raise significant risks, including infringement of the right to privacy and personal data, bias and discrimination against marginalized groups, security vulnerabilities, and potential regulatory gaps. Establishing comprehensive governance frameworks and rules is imperative to ensure that the use of these intelligence systems aligns with ethical standards and legal systems, focusing on fundamental principles like transparency, integrity, fairness, and accountability. The lack of equitable technological capabilities among nations, coupled with the concentration of control over AI systems in a small number of companies, further compounds existing inequalities, underscoring the necessity for robust international regulatory mechanisms to prevent harm.

The research defines AI governance and identifies the responsible parties while studying existing global initiatives, including the Shanghai Declaration, the OECD AI Principles, UNESCO's AI Ethics Recommendation, and notably, the European AI Act, which was recently approved as a global first for AI regulation. Core to these governance frameworks is the mandate for transparency, meaning that the data, algorithms, capabilities, processes, and purpose of an AI system must be explainable to those affected by automated decision-making. Continuous monitoring and evaluation of AI systems are crucial to identifying and correcting bias, thereby promoting fairness in decision-making processes. However, policymakers face a challenge given the rapid technological progress and the unpredictability of AI development, suggesting that overly inflexible regulations enacted today may quickly become ineffective or irrelevant.

Establishing comprehensive frameworks for AI governance requires broad cooperation from all parties, institutions, and international bodies—governmental, private, and civil—at both regional and international levels. To this end, the paper strongly recommends holding an international conference under the auspices of the United Nations, attended by the world's largest technology companies, to develop globally applicable governance rules. Ultimately, this work serves as a foundational study, paving the way for further research into specific AI governance challenges, such as specialized privacy protection, transparency mechanisms, and the continued development of adaptive frameworks capable of keeping pace with the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence technologies.
