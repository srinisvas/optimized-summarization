Executive Summary

The rapidly evolving landscape of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) integrated with vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communications within the forthcoming 6G networks presents significant challenges to ensuring adequate privacy protection. This research addresses the privacy risks introduced by this advanced technological integration by developing and evaluating AI-driven predictive privacy mechanisms. The core methodology involves training sophisticated machine learning algorithms to analyze large volumes of data transferred across V2X-6G networks, enabling the systems to identify patterns indicative of privacy threats, such as unauthorized data access or information leakage. These predictive privacy tools allow for immediate adjustments in security measures, empowering systems to respond proactively by autonomously adjusting privacy settings or alerting users in real-time, thereby permitting organizations to fortify their defenses preemptively.

While predictive privacy powered by AI has many advantages, including the efficient processing of large datasets while maintaining user privacy through automated systems that adhere to regulations, this integration also introduces significant ethical concerns and difficulties that must be thoroughly investigated. A critical concern is the potential for AI biases, as the accuracy, completeness, and lack of bias in the training data used to make decisions might cause AI systems to provide incorrect results. Addressing these ethical implications is crucial to fostering responsible and equitable AI-driven privacy solutions, particularly concerning the contentious issue of the fine line between monitoring for security purposes and infringing on personal privacy, which necessitates improving AI models’ interpretability and explainability.

In conclusion, the integration of artificial intelligence for predictive privacy marks a significant advancement in addressing the security risks inherent in UAV-integrated V2X-6G networks by enhancing data security and user trust. However, the study underscores that ethical frameworks are required to address the ramifications of AI usage, alongside the investigation of user-centric privacy measures that enable individuals to personalize their settings. Future work should aim to improve AI algorithms to limit biases, increase predictive measures, and create real-time adaptability to risks that change over time, while also exploring the long-term efficacy and acceptability of these systems and their potential compatibility with upcoming technologies such as blockchain.
