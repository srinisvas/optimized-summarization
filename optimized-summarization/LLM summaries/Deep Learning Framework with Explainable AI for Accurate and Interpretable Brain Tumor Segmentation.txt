Executive Summary

This research addresses the critical need for accurate and timely brain tumor detection via MRI, confronting the limitations of traditional deep learning models which, despite high accuracy, function as "black-box" systems that lack the interpretability essential for clinical trust, alongside the inherent privacy hurdles associated with sharing sensitive patient data across medical institutions. The paper proposes an integrated deep learning framework that incorporates Federated Learning (FL) for privacy preservation and Explainable AI (XAI), specifically utilizing SHAP and LIME techniques, to generate transparent and interpretable predictions. This approach allows institutions to collaboratively train a global model using the Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm by aggregating locally computed updates, without exchanging raw patient data, thus bridging AI research to reliable, privacy-preserving clinical applications.

Evaluated using an MRI scan dataset, the proposed system demonstrated robust performance metrics, achieving an accuracy of 96.8%, sensitivity of 96.5%, specificity of 96.4%, and an F1-score of 96.6%. These results confirm that the integrated system outperforms traditional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) models in terms of reliability and diagnostic accuracy while successfully ensuring data privacy through FL. The incorporation of XAI ensures that physicians receive explainable explanations for every decision, thereby fostering confidence in the system’s output and allowing the system’s output to be forwarded for clinical assessment.

Despite these technological advancements, the current framework faces limitations primarily related to computational cost and transmission overhead. Federated learning is a computationally demanding operation that results in longer training timeframes and increased processing demands, especially during model aggregation, which challenges the system's real-time processing capability. Furthermore, the generation of detailed explanations via XAI adds to the computational expense. Future work is directed toward optimizing the FL framework to reduce communication overhead and improve aggregation speed, alongside efforts to enhance model robustness and generalization by incorporating bigger and more diverse datasets from multiple institutions to account for data heterogeneity. The long-term goal emphasizes optimizing the system for seamless clinical integration, focusing on advanced XAI methods to reduce computational costs for faster explanation generation to provide fast, accurate predictions tailored for efficient clinical workflows.
