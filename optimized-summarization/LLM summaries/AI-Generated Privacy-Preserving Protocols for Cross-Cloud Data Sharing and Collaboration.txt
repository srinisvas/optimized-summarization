**Executive Summary**

The rapid expansion of cloud computing has driven organizations toward higher dependence on several cloud platforms to meet divergent storage, computation, and collaboration needs, yet conventional methods for securing cross-cloud data sharing suffer from scalability and performance problems, especially since computation-intensive solutions like homomorphic encryption are often impractical for large-scale deployment. This research focuses on designing and implementing AI-generated privacy-preserving protocols to improve data security in these heterogeneous cross-cloud environments, ensuring the efficient use of computing powers while addressing the challenge of standardizing privacy practices across various regulatory jurisdictions. The proposed solution leverages machine learning algorithms for runtime risk detection and dynamic flow encryption guided by predefined privacy policies, optimizing shared model accuracy while adhering to data protection regulations.

The developed architecture integrates several interdependent modules, including federated learning, which enables secure decentralized model training by sharing only model updates rather than raw data, thus ensuring data confidentiality across diverse cloud platforms and minimizing associated privacy risks. For enhanced security at the individual record level, differential privacy methods are utilized by adding statistical noise to prevent the reverse engineering of data points. A critical component is the dynamic encryption engine, which continuously monitors contextual parameters such as user roles, geographic location, and application usage, allowing for varying levels of encryption and updating security policies based on the sensitivity of detected data and threats, such as when data is transferred across borders versus within a regulatory-compliant region. Further optimization includes adaptive resource management, where AI models facilitate runtime allocations or reallocations based on workload and timely analysis of threats.

These AI-generated protocols were experimentally deployed on a simulated multicloud testbed comprising three major cloud providers with different security policies. The empirical results indicate that the proposed protocols significantly outperform state-of-the-art methods in maintaining data integrity, minimizing leakage risks, and enhancing data interoperability, with key metrics focused on the percentage of simulated attacks detected and contained (Attack Resistance). The findings confirm the effectiveness of the protocols in achieving higher privacy scores and attack resistance compared to traditional methods, while simultaneously optimizing resource utilization and keeping latency low. This confirmation of applicability in real-world multi-cloud scenarios validates the study's contribution toward strengthening secure collaboration processes across various verticals, including healthcare, finance, and telecommunications, without incurring a performance penalty.
