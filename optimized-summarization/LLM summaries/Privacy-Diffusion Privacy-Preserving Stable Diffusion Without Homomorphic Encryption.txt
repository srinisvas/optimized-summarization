**Executive Summary**
Text-to-image generation, particularly using state-of-the-art open-source models like Stable Diffusion, presents significant privacy challenges regarding the protection of users’ input text prompts, the generated output images, and the customized models maintained by service providers on the server. Protecting intellectual property and ensuring user trust necessitates robust privacy protocols, but existing solutions often employ Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE), which typically leads to substantial computational overhead—such as a 200% increase in execution time—and visible degradation of image quality. This paper introduces Privacy-Diffusion, a novel framework designed to protect both client and server privacy in diffusion models without the need for FHE or differential privacy.

The Privacy-Diffusion framework achieves its security goals by leveraging the irreversible properties inherent in neural network layers and capitalizing on the characteristic that the predicted noise in the diffusion process conforms to a normalized Gaussian distribution. This approach successfully protects clients' input text prompts and generated images from potential compromise by the server, while simultaneously safeguarding the customized model parameters from unauthorized access by clients. This protocol, implemented using the Stable Diffusion model v1.4 with a DDIM scheduler at 512x512 resolution, effectively addresses the dual privacy concerns present in the generative AI pipeline.

Comparative benchmarking demonstrates that Privacy-Diffusion reaches an equivalent security level to prior FHE-based research but achieves drastically improved efficiency. The framework imposes only a 4% extra time overhead, a marked improvement over the 200% overhead reported by existing methods like HEdiffusion, and maintains high-quality image output with absolutely no discernible quality loss, as it avoids using approximations or differential privacy mechanisms. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first framework to achieve privacy-preserving Stable Diffusion without relying on FHE while maintaining high-quality image generation, and the methodology can be readily extended to other generative machine learning models exhibiting similar structural characteristics.
