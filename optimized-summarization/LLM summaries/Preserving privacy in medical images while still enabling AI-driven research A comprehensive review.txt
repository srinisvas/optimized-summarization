**Executive Summary**

The proliferation of AI algorithms in medical image processing necessitates access to vast datasets containing protected health information (PHI), creating a fundamental conflict between research utility and legal mandates for privacy preservation, such as HIPAA and GDPR. While various techniques exist to preserve individual privacy, the authors note that the majority of state-of-the-art studies have primarily focused on structured medical data or developing defense mechanisms against adversarial attacks. Given the acknowledged ethical and legal necessity to protect patient data—despite the fact that fully anonymizing medical images without compromising their utility is currently impossible, leaving some level of re-identification risk—this paper conducts a comprehensive protocol-driven review across three large databases (ScienceDirect, ACM, PubMed) focusing specifically on unstructured medical image data.

This review provides an overview of the techniques and tools adopted for medical image de-identification or anonymization, alongside the limitations faced by researchers in ensuring patient privacy. Key findings indicate that anonymization, defined as the irreversible process of ensuring personal information cannot be used to re-identify a patient, is paramount. Preferred solutions include the use of image de-facer programs to distort all or partial facial features, as well as the protection of demographics and diagnosis codes, which are vital for researchers conducting medical studies. However, the analysis of selected relevant papers revealed implementation issues, such as researchers encoding acquisition parameters into private attributes or misusing attribute fields for their intended purposes, suggesting that DICOM de-identification solutions require evaluation and quality checks before deployment.

To guide future open healthcare data sharing initiatives, the authors formulated a seven-recommendation approach, emphasizing that PHI must be correctly documented and conformance statements clearly defined when utilizing specific de-identification or anonymization approaches. The literature review also identified innovative solutions designed to combat specific threats, including the PRICURE system, which was introduced to prevent membership inference attacks while maintaining the accuracy of models, and systems that combine secure multi-party computation and differential privacy to enable private multiple model prediction. This body of work underscores that medical AI-driven research is actively striving for better solutions to maintain data utility while keeping patients' privacy intact, confirming that these issues remain a critical area for future research.
