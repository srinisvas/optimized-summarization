**Executive Summary**

Extended Reality (XR) technology is demonstrating significant benefit to patients and healthcare professionals, with the healthcare sector emerging as one of the largest industries to adopt this technology globally. XR use-cases span crucial areas, including assisting surgeons in performing complex operations, immersing patients and clinicians in educational materials, and providing specialized training within virtual environments. However, the increasing integration of XR-based medical applications necessitates a robust ethical framework, which must be evolved from best practices across both medical and technological fields to ensure the safe and equitable usage of the technology. A fundamental requirement for enhancing the value of any healthcare technology product is building in privacy and security protections from the outset, thereby securing collected information and ensuring data usage and disclosure is strictly limited to what is approved or expected by the user.

Clinicians and researchers are currently leveraging this technology in critical areas, such as using XR-based platforms for neuropsychological assessments and evaluating the efficacy or response to ongoing therapies, including for cancer treatment. Specific tools like The Body VR are being utilized to construct 3D models from radiologic imaging data (CT, MRI, PET scans) to aid pre-procedural planning for patients with cardiovascular diseases, while similar applications are being developed to help patients identify and avoid substance misuse. While emerging virtual reality systems offer intriguing therapeutic possibilities, their development must be guided by ethical priorities that account for specific patient vulnerabilities, strictly adhering to the core bioethical principles of respect for autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, and justice, especially as XR environments may influence how people perceive themselves or others.

The ethical deployment of Medical XR requires an urgent focus on policy and design, emphasizing the need for robust data collection protocols and clear government policies relating to the technology. Key ethical principles addressed include equitable and sustainable usage of Medical XR, designing for inclusivity and accessibility, and ensuring informed consent, which is contingent upon the healthcare professional’s duty to provide patients with correct information regarding their condition and available therapeutic options. Considering accessibility as a core part of a system's iterative design process is vital, as it benefits all users who may experience situational disabilities based on their context. Therefore, an effective ethical approach dictates a society-generated design methodology to manage the inherent risks and challenges of this rapidly advancing technology.
