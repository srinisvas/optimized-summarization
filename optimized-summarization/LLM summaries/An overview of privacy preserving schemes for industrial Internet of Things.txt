**Executive Summary**
The fundamental transformation of production spurred by Industry 4.0 aims for deep integration of traditional industrial platforms with intelligent technologies like big data and artificial intelligence, necessitating the interconnection of factories and machines for more efficient production. However, this shift generates vast amounts of raw data—including customer behavior characteristics, production detail scheduling, and device running status—which constitutes private information vulnerable to illegal mining, undisclosed commercial use, or even public sale, seriously damaging enterprise interests. Addressing this critical security requirement, this comprehensive overview investigates the privacy issues and related solutions for both the cloud-based and edge-based industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) architectures. The study categorizes privacy concerns across four key aspects: data, location, identity, and query issues.

Although a cloud server offers powerful computing and storage capabilities to perform highly complex calculations, it suffers from high bandwidth requirements and significant time delays, compounded by the difficulty of network bandwidth satisfying cloud-based transmission rates due to the exponential growth of data volume. Conversely, privacy issues in the edge-based industrial IoT architecture are explored across three specific scenarios: a typical edge-based IIoT system, an SDN enabled edge IIoT system, and an edge IIoT system with blockchain. Solutions investigated include typical methods to anonymize sensitive data, such as K-anonymity, L-diversity, T-closeness, and artificial intelligence methods, as well as specific schemes like PRIME-Q for multi-domain SDN industrial IoT, which addresses potential privacy leakage during cross-domain collaboration. In terms of complexity, the secure local binary pattern descriptor extraction scheme for image data privacy, for example, has been shown to reach a complexity of $O(n^2)$.

The principal contribution of this work is a detailed comparison of the privacy protection performance and the computational complexity of various existing privacy solutions across both cloud and edge architectures. This analysis allows for a clear understanding of the advantages and disadvantages of each method. By detailing solutions that can hide real location and effectively resist location privacy attacks, or cooperative multiparty systems where each party only knows its own data and the final aggregated result, the paper serves as a valuable reference and guideline for further privacy investigation on industrial IoT systems, concluding with an outline of the open issues that remain in current studies.
