**Executive Summary**
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is identified as a disruptive, utility-portable, and probabilistic technology presenting significant challenges to entrepreneurial ethics, socio-legal frameworks, and political evolution within globalized cyberspace. The inherent difficulties arise because existing legal frameworks often rely on mono-truistic and absolutist interpretations of positive law, which are deemed insufficient to address AI's complex systemic nature and its "alien nature of legal empathy." To address this gap, the paper introduces a doctrinal understanding centered on the **Entitative Model of Artificial Intelligence (EAAI)**, coupled with the analytical proposition of **Dimensional Perpetuity (DP)**, positioning it as a necessary legal and ontological ethic to cultivate a naturalist approach toward AI's legal persona.

The research proposes that an AI, as a Legal Personality, possesses two distinctive approaches: the Utilitarian Approach (UAAI) and the Entitative Approach (EAAI). Central to DP is the parameter of Self-transformability, which signifies the Self-Transformative Nature of AI and helps estimate its multifaceted attributes by acknowledging the underlying algorithmic design and its natural flow. The related concept, the **Realm of Dimensional Perpetuity (RDP)**, is based on substantive parameters designed to outline the infrastructure's legal ecosystem, thus providing a preliminary framework to embrace the ontological sides of AI’s legal persona. This framework is crucial because traditional concepts like differential privacy—which focus on anonymizing the identity of requested information—are insufficient to address the fundamental legal disruption caused by AI.

The conclusions emphasize that to manage the consequential implications of AI’s disruption, there must be a necessary shift in jurisprudential thinking from anthropocentrism and absolutism toward anthropomorphism and naturalism. For technology companies and data-driven governments, this means business strategies must move away from opaque and blind algorithmic tactics in their statistics-oriented appropriation of information, instead encouraging stable ethical means to render AI communicable and explainable. The adoption of the EAAI and RDP assists in naturalizing AI with human society, ensuring that the development of technology fosters trust and cultivates a more inclusive and connotating relationship between AI, as a special human artifact, and data subjects.
