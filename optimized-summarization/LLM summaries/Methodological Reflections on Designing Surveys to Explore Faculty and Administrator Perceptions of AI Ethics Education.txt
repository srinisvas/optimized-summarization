Executive Summary
This paper details the methodological reflections surrounding the design of two distinct surveys developed to explore faculty and administrator perceptions of AI ethics education. Recognizing that the extent and mechanisms of effectiveness or ineffectiveness of existing ethics education interventions remain largely unexplored, the research team created one survey for faculty members and another for administrators. The faculty survey, specifically, includes a section aimed at understanding the institutional context, including the kind of support faculty experience and their awareness of AI ethics education policies, with the goal of determining if a lack of opportunity, resources, skills, pedagogical constraints, or other barriers are impeding the development of AI ethics interventions at the national scale.

Survey design was identified as a complex process, requiring a twofold solution to address challenges such as multiple accessibility concerns raised during development. The authors aimed for a "Goldilocks approach" that successfully balances specific options and presented arrays of choices to participants along with free text responses to capture meaningful specificity and difference. To maintain data fidelity when questioning faculty about multiple courses, the design utilized the built-in functionality of Qualtrics to track which courses a person had already provided answers for, minimizing the likelihood that repetitiveness and fatigue would degrade answers. Furthermore, the researchers drew from the National Center of Education Statistics for guidance to avoid an arbitrary classification of institutional sizes, ensuring the contextual data was robust.

The administrator survey, which was built upon Beever et al., had fewer versions and was written more quickly, having been developed as a pilot study to share with partners at regional hubs for feedback as part of the developmental process. The overall project remains mindful of the larger picture, maintaining alignment with funding goals and considering partner networks as future collaborators. The research ultimately seeks to clarify the institutional level: how faculty perceive institutional efforts to implement and measure ethics in programs, departments, and institutions, concluding that the methodological rigor applied is necessary for producing effective instruments that gather meaningful data while managing participant burden.
