Executive Summary

This research addresses the critical intersection of strategic Information Systems (IS) procurement, emerging AI governance frameworks, and practical tools for implementing AI ethics, acknowledging the strategic shift of IS procurement due to continuously rising IT investments. The core inquiry investigated how existing tools and methods in AI ethics relate to procurement practices and how procurement is positioned within proposed AI governance frameworks. The study contributed by defining and utilizing six types of documents as main categories for content analysis, helping to establish more structured use of critical terms within AI ethics research. Using a governance structure for human-centered AI as a theoretical background, the study positioned IS procurement practices in the middle layer, seeing these as integral to organizational management strategies.

The analysis confirmed the hypothesis that tangible tools and methods designed specifically for IS procurement are scarce. Initial review revealed that most sources were either general research documents or technical implementation models focused on algorithm-based solutions, confirming a low number of documents categorized as practical methods or tools. A subsequent detailed analysis of the finalized data set indicated that only four individual documents, representing 12.5% of the relevant collection, had a direct link to procurement practices. This finding provides new information highlighting that the academic field, despite being the leading producer of methods and tools in AI ethics, has practically dismissed the procurement context, which is considered an alarming issue given the critical and strategic role of IS procurement in the success of system development and implementation within both public and private sectors.

Analysis based on the hierarchical governance structure demonstrated a solid connection where the higher the organizational level a document is intended for, the more abstract the document content becomes, suggesting a need for tangible practices across all governance levels, not just the operational team level where most current tools reside. However, the hierarchical approach also suggests a positive outcome: since documents positioned at each level share similar intended target groups, entirely new methods strictly for IS procurement might not be necessary. Instead, the study proposes further analysis of existing methods and tools targeted at organizational management to explore their adaptability for procurement practitioners. This research contributes by establishing a preliminary understanding of the limited linkage between the currently independent fields of AI governance, AI ethics, and IS procurement.
