**Executive Summary**

This article addresses the critical challenge of implementing a Privacy Preserving Data Publishing (PPDP) scheme that maximizes data utility while guaranteeing robust privacy, a necessary balance for futuristic AI applications and Responsible Data Sharing (RDS). Traditional mechanisms often anonymize entire sections of data, failing to maximally preserve the structure and statistical values of real data; this failure leads to anonymized data lacking functional relationships similar to real data, inadvertently propagating biases that cause AI models to make biased or incorrect decisions. The proposed solution is a pioneer hybrid anonymity scheme that integrates Differential Privacy (DP) with k-anonymity, ensuring sufficient resilience against privacy attacks by identifying and applying necessary anonymization only within privacy-violating partitions.

The methodological innovation centers on selectively processing Quasi-Identifiers (QIDs) based on their partition status. Unlike prior methods, this scheme uses distinct values for the privacy budget $\epsilon$, significantly reducing the offset in numerical QID values between real and anonymized data. Categorical QIDs are released "as is" in non-privacy-violating partitions, and are only minimally generalized in the privacy-violating partition by exploiting Sensitive Attribute (SA) diversity information, which leverages the fact that implementing k-anonymity has the greatest impact on the overall method. This selective attention to privacy-violating data (e.g., super minor values) allows the scheme to extract and preserve most parts of the data in their original form, achieving strong privacy guarantees while keeping the data highly usable.

The implementation results, validated using benchmark datasets, confirmed that the proposed scheme significantly outperformed State-of-the-Art (SOTA) methods across multiple metrics, including originality preservation, ensuring higher utility in the anonymized data, and providing superior ability to defend against privacy breaches. Furthermore, the scheme demonstrated significantly lowered computing overhead, making it applicable even in resource-constrained environments. By preserving high data quality and statistical information, the scheme resolves the urgent need to address data-specific biases and creates anonymized data most suitable for data-hungry AI applications.
