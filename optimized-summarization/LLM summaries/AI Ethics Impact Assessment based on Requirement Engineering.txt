**Executive Summary**
This paper proposes the AI Ethics Impact Assessment, a structured methodology designed to bridge the gap between abstract AI ethics principles and practical implementation by AI service providers, developers, and users. Recognizing that existing ethical guidelines pose a challenge for practical application within AI systems, the research assumes, based on a survey of past AI ethical incident cases, that potential ethical risks are inherently mapped to the interactions occurring between the components of an AI system and the stakeholders directly or indirectly involved. This methodology aims to comprehensively extract these potential ethical risks in accordance with established AI ethics guidelines, such as those that designate certain applications, like the use of personal biometrics or critical infrastructure applications, as "high-risk AI."

The methodology is comprised of two core approaches: first, the development of an AI ethics model that embodies ethical guidelines as necessary requirements and correlates them with system interactions using requirement engineering; notably, the second and lower layers of this model are structured from the Assessment Lists of the Trustworthy AI (ALTAI). Second, the process includes a multi-step impact assessment, mechanically carried out, that uses this model to extract ethical risks for individual AI systems. This procedure provides a framework for AI service providers to consider the risk assessment and control of their AI services, enabling developers, providers, and users without specific expertise in ethical guidelines to assess risks across the entire AI lifecycle, from planning and development to operation and retirement.

An initial validation of the methodology was conducted via a questionnaire survey to test the core assumption that ethical risks are associated with system interactions. The results suggested the validity of this foundational assumption, confirming that participants with knowledge of AI ethics generally agreed on the nature of the identified risks and the associated interactions. However, the authors acknowledge that these results are not sufficient for full validation, citing issues with the small number of participants and a bias toward those who already possess knowledge of AI ethics. Therefore, future work is deemed necessary to improve the verification methods and involve a wider range of participants to reliably validate and improve the proposed methodology.
