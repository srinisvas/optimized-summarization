**Executive Summary**

The era of artificial intelligence (AI) has marked a pivotal point in technological development, necessitating that companies ensure their AI systems are both technologically advanced and ethically responsible by building trust and ensuring sustainability. Implementing Privacy by Design is established as the fundamental framework for integrating privacy into the very heart of AI technology from its conceptualization to its deployment, embodying the principle of proactivity—being preventive rather than corrective—to avoid security breaches and ensure privacy is an intrinsic component rather than an afterthought. This approach is particularly crucial given the unique privacy challenges posed by AI, which include managing the algorithmic opacity inherent in many systems, mitigating the specific issue of re-identification that differs from other information systems, and addressing resilience to specialized attacks such as "data poisoning." These advanced protection measures require a focus on data minimization, ensuring personal data collection is minimized by default, and that any information collected is rigorously protected and restricted to only necessary information for specific purposes.

To achieve these goals, companies must prioritize transparency by clearly communicating how they collect, use, and share customer data, a strategy that aligns with GDPR requirements and fosters necessary trust in AI systems. This commitment involves implementing mechanisms of transparency and explainability from the design phase, which allows for a clear understanding of how algorithms make decisions and the ability to explain those decisions to affected users. Furthermore, companies must provide customers with choices regarding data use, ensuring they have control over their personal information, and allowing them to access, correct, and delete their data. Implementing robust security measures and conducting regular security audits are essential to protect AI systems, which should be designed to automatically respect user privacy without requiring additional intervention.

Finally, integrating Privacy by Design requires continuous assessment and accountability. Companies must embrace the responsibility of protecting customer data and be accountable for any breaches, necessitating comprehensive audits and analyses of their data practices to continuously strive for improved privacy measures. Creating controlled testing environments for AI models, where different threat scenarios are simulated, is also necessary to allow developers to identify and correct potential vulnerabilities before system deployment. Looking ahead, effectively incorporating these principles into AI will be a key hallmark of companies that not only thrive but also lead the way in the era of digital transformation, following guidelines (such as those from the OECD report, 2023) that mandate clear guidelines for interoperability and ethical data handling.
