Executive Summary
Cloud computing technology is widely adopted globally due to its advantages, such as providing users with on-demand access to applications and personal data; however, persistent privacy concerns and issues often discourage users from fully engaging in the cloud computing environment (CCE). Recognizing that previous information system literature primarily addressed information privacy concern as a general worry reflecting users’ natural anxieties about the potential for information privacy loss, this paper adopts a quantitative research approach to specifically investigate the factors influencing information privacy concern within CCE. Data was collected using a quantitative survey methodology, yielding a total of 340 usable cases gathered from a sampled population of students. The research assessed the influence of three independent variables—perceived privacy risk, perceived privacy control, and perceived effectiveness of privacy policy—on the dependent variable, information privacy concern.

The statistical analysis, supported by an ANOVA demonstrating that the model is significant at 99%, revealed that perceived privacy risk is statistically and significantly influencing information privacy concern in the cloud computing environment. This finding supports the consensus that risk is a vital factor in making online decisions, suggesting that when users feel a sense of privacy risk, their concern for information privacy is consequently high. Conversely, the study found that both perceived privacy control and perceived effectiveness of privacy policy have no significant impact on information privacy concern in CCE. The researchers suggest that this divergence from some prior studies, particularly regarding the role of perceived privacy control, might be attributable to differences in the targeted population, noting that this study focused on students at the International Islamic University while related studies focused on large universities in the United States.

Furthermore, contextual analysis revealed that social networking services (including Facebook, LinkedIn, MySpace, Twitter, Instagram, and WhatsApp) had the highest percentage of usage rate among the study participants. The successful determination that perceived privacy risk is a key factor influencing CCE concern accomplished the primary objective of the study. Consequently, the research recommends that cloud service providers should focus on improving the quality of cloud service delivery to address privacy gaps and must establish clear privacy risk management plans to effectively reduce users' information privacy concerns.
