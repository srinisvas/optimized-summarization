Executive Summary

This paper introduces a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) decision support tool (PIA DSS tool) specifically designed for integration within cloud computing environments, addressing the critical need for proactive privacy evaluation in systems where actual or perceived privacy weaknesses directly impact user trust, legal compliance, and data security. The PIA is defined as a systematic process for evaluating the possible future effects that a particular activity may have on individual privacy, serving as a proactive business process distinguished from reactive measures like privacy audits or law compliance checking applied to existing systems. The research posits that no such dedicated PIA application exists for the cloud environment and aims to bridge this gap by offering a generic approach believed to be of increasing benefit as cloud service adoption increases.

The PIA tool provides essential decision support by using a rule-based system in a controlled way, enabling organizations to navigate the complex international PIA landscape. Typically, PIAs involve a series of steps focused on posing and answering questions and considering options, a structure that the tool facilitates. The system’s primary function is to address the complexity of privacy compliance requirements by highlighting privacy risks and compliance issues for individuals within the organization who are not privacy and security experts, allowing them to identify suitable solutions in a given situation. The tool indicates levels of risk associated with different categories using a color code system, where black signifies high risk, grey equates to a warning, and white indicates low risk associated with that category.

Developed using Java components (such as Java Server Pages and JavaBeans), the architecture of the tool allows it to store sensitive data on its own data center while making use of public and private cloud services for general computing, often employing a hybrid cloud or 'cloudburst' model. By surfacing privacy issues at an early stage and providing relevant knowledge, the PIA tool facilitates the raising of a system's privacy baseline at the architectural level without causing undue impact on functionality. This approach can help decision makers determine whether a new project should proceed. Future extensions of the approach may also incorporate mechanisms to provide enhanced proof that the user information utilized by the PIA tool is correct, specifically by checking the location of information stored and transferred in association with the assessed cloud services.
