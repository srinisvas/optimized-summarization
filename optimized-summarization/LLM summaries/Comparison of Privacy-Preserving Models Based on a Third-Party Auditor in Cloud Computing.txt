Executive Summary

This paper provides a detailed comparison and recapitulation of the state-of-the-art Privacy Preserving Models (PPMs) within cloud computing (CC), specifically evaluating cloud security concerns when utilizing the services of a Third-Party Auditor (TPA). Cloud computing is defined as a utility-driven, "pay as you use" technology vastly utilized across domains, but its adoption requires users to consider inherent risks, particularly since dynamic operations can compromise auditing protocols. While the TPA is intended to assure auditing functions and secure data integrity on behalf of the client, the core issue explored is the emergence of trust concerns, extra overhead, and the potential for security breaches and data manipulation that arise from TPA involvement, especially given that TPAs often have control of private keys, which is deemed unsafe.

The research pursued its goal by studying the most recent TPA-based approaches, classifying them according to their adopted security methods, and detailing the vulnerabilities of CC, revealing different scenarios where the TPA's adoption could generate threats on data privacy. These vulnerabilities include specific attacks such as the Intoxication Attack, where users misbehave after earning trust, and Self-Promoting behaviors intended to promote a Cloud service provider. Although the novel TPA scheme ensures data confidentiality using encryption techniques and secure authentication, the analysis reveals that this security often comes with large verification overhead. The PPMs were classified and categorized, with a focus on TPA dynamicity, and the limitations of each model were extensively discussed.

Simulations were conducted using the Greencloud platform, comparing the four most eminent PPMs against the previously introduced LAPP method. This comparison revealed the major impact brought in by TPA’s adoption in securing CC, while simultaneously confirming the associated concerns regarding trust and complexity, such as high management complexity in the hybrid cloud. Based on this comprehensive comparison, the paper concludes with the recommendation for researchers to develop a more simplistic, lightweight, and secure solution than LAPP. This recommended future work should be oriented toward alleviating the decision compromise faced by cloud users and ultimately increasing their trust in adopting TPA-based solutions.
