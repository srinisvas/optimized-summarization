**Executive Summary**

Power utilities are increasingly transitioning to information-driven Smart Grids that rely on bi-directional communication for distributed energy management. While Cloud environments offer advantages like elastic resources and shared services well-suited for Smart Grid applications, deploying these systems on the Cloud significantly exacerbates inherent security and privacy concerns due to extensive information flows. This research provides a taxonomical analysis of the security and privacy issues within a Smart Grid software architecture operating across various Cloud environments, utilizing the ongoing Los Angeles Smart Grid Project—the largest U.S. municipal utility demonstration—to drive the investigative context. The analysis accounts for stakeholder interactions, user roles, and the distinct security and privacy challenges arising from these interactions, with utilities often providing infrastructure for third-party services to access consumer and other data available in the Cloud, sometimes involving Private Clouds dedicated to a single organization.

The complexity stems from the nature and volume of data, which includes fine-grained usage information, such as bytes/kilobytes from Home Area Network (HAN) systems reported through smart meters, and high-frequency power quality measurements from phasor measurement units. The increasing chance that personally identifiable information will be passed requires careful handling to prevent privacy violations, especially since information acquired and integrated by the utility must be preserved over long time periods, mirroring issues previously encountered in the life sciences domain under HIPAA regulation. Importantly, while some privacy concerns arise from inadequate security, others are the side effect of integrating disparate data sources—including consumers, public agencies, and online service providers—which together may provide unprecedented insights into user activities, affecting traditional electricity forecast and planning models.

The paper’s primary contribution is a taxonomy that classifies various contributing factors and user roles associated with Cloud security and privacy issues within the Smart Grid domain, organizing known security concerns from a practitioner's perspective. The analysis categorizes attacks on the Smart Grid into three areas: network availability, data integrity, and information privacy. Utilities in the United States are simultaneously regulated by federal and state energy regulatory commissions, adding layers of policy complexity. Among potential mitigation strategies, some solutions implement Smart Grid security as a Software as a Service (SaaS) model, with communications passing through access control and intrusion detection services, while others promote a user-centric approach for privacy management. Ultimately, this taxonomy identifies several unique privacy and regulatory issues posing challenges for further research and is intended to guide developers and researchers in building secure and privacy-preserving Smart Grid applications.
