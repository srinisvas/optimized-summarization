Executive Summary
Technological evolution has historically generated less desired side effects with societal impact, particularly within IT&C and digital media systems, where issues such as misinformation, manipulation, and the dissemination of fake news are prevalent. The expanding applicability of artificial intelligence (AI) across diverse sectors—including health services, education, transportation, e-commerce, and defense—necessitates the urgent addressing of new issues related to ethics, safety, human control, and regulatory elements. To address these challenges and protect citizens' rights and freedoms, the European Union has promoted legislative and normative documents, exemplified by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which outlines fundamental compliance concepts such as legality, fairness, transparency, limitation of purpose, data minimization, accuracy, and integrity and confidentiality.

Despite the existence of regulatory frameworks, software developers retain autonomy to create software systems based on their needs, allowing for practices where data is retrieved from anywhere and published, and potentially transferred to non-interested entities. Responsibility, defined as the ability to demonstrate respect for principles, is complicated by storage limitations and the difficulty of controlling phenomena that affect both system management and users. The current form of AI is already becoming a part of the decision-making process, highlighting the importance of documents like the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative and the National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence, which deal extensively with the subject of AI ethics and the need for positive development of behavior and attitude.

When analyzing the objectives of information systems, user rights under GDPR—such as the right to information, correction of inaccurate personal data, and the right to deletion of user data—are paramount. However, the procedures required to implement these rights are often cited as difficult, if not impossible, to execute in practice. Furthermore, the goal of achieving democratic access to cultural information stored in national and international digital collections without discrimination reinforces the ethical imperative for transparent and controllable systems, requiring strict adherence to data protection principles and ensuring that AI development remains subordinate to human rights.
