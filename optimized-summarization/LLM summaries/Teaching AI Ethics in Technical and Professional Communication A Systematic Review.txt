This systematic literature review assesses the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) literacy and AI ethics within Technical and Professional Communication (TPC), documenting how these crucial concepts have been studied by the field. Due to the current narrow selection of relevant literature within TPC, the study relied on interdisciplinary, adjacent fields where AI ethics and literacy education were already implemented to establish its thematic scope. Employing a hermeneutic methodology, the research involved repeated cycles of searching, filtering, and interpretation across wide-ranging academic sources, resulting in the critical assessment of 32 articles published between 2017 and 2022 that covered various case studies, frameworks, and pedagogical activities. This process involved writing notes in a memo format to reflect on emerging themes and patterns, supporting an analytic design focused on challenging assumptions regarding instructional content, with all categories remaining mutually exclusive.

The analysis confirms recent trends advocating for a redesign of educational programs to more intentionally adopt AI ethics in pedagogy, driven by the fact that AI technology is evolving quickly and introducing a "responsibility gap" concerning accountability for outcomes, requiring efforts that go beyond mere criticism. To address this, the authors propose a three-level framework—consisting of institutional, course, and instruction levels—designed to align AI ethics literacy with program objectives and outcomes. Content at the pedagogical level specifically addresses concerns regarding the most effective approach for teaching theoretical components. Although varied, the current sources demonstrate a range of instructional content and assignments that encourage continual engagement and reflection, demonstrating the ability to integrate varying activities; however, implementing such coursework currently faces challenges due to limited partnerships between practitioners in academia and industry. Ultimately, the reviewed literature emphasizes that AI ethics and AI literacy have considerable overlap, necessitating a combined, interdisciplinary approach in teaching efforts.

The primary contribution of this research is setting the groundwork for pedagogies and best practices that prepare technical communicators to evaluate AI technologies from ethical perspectives, concluding that a systematic approach to AI pedagogy allows TPC instructors to leverage existing resources effectively. A course relevant to industry must prepare students with appropriate experience to strategically understand, use, and evaluate AI technology. Further application of ethical theory that indirectly relates to AI ethics must rigorously consider potential biases, and future research should concentrate on expanding pedagogical approaches and drawing connections between AI ethics and specific TPC theories, particularly social justice and audience analysis.
