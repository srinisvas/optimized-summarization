**Executive Summary**

Addressing the inherent limitations of centralized air quality monitoring—specifically high latency, bandwidth consumption, and neglect of data privacy—this research describes a novel decentralized system utilizing Edge AI and sophisticated machine learning techniques. The methodology involved outfitting IoT sensors with NVIDIA Jetson devices deployed across 50 urban sites characterized by varied pollution levels. These local devices continuously monitor and analyze key contaminants, including PM2.5, CO2, and NOx, in real time using powerful machine learning models, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). This architectural shift ensures that data processing, analysis, and prediction occur at the edge, rather than relying on constant transmission to a central cloud server.

The comprehensive evaluation of the decentralized system demonstrated significant performance advantages over traditional cloud-based systems. The proposed approach achieved a substantial reduction in latency, cutting delays by up to 75%, and successfully reduced bandwidth utilization by 60%, thereby minimizing operational costs and enhancing scalability. The efficiency of the machine learning models in pollutant detection was confirmed by detailed comparisons and validations against common reference monitors, yielding a high average correlation coefficient of 0.92 across all monitored contaminants and locations, demonstrating high accuracy and reliability in delivering real-time air quality data.

A critical feature of this system is the integration of federated learning, which addresses inherent data privacy concerns often ignored in conventional monitoring frameworks. This technique ensures that raw data remains on local devices, as each Edge AI device changes its model locally based on collected information, rather than transmitting sensitive raw data to a central server. This strategy solves privacy concerns while simultaneously ensuring continual model improvement through local updates and global model aggregation, resulting in a steady increase in model accuracy of up to 15% over a six-month period. Overall, this research demonstrates the utility of decentralized air quality monitoring systems that effectively leverage Edge AI and federated learning to provide efficient, accurate, and privacy-preserving solutions essential for public health and environmental protection in urban environments.
