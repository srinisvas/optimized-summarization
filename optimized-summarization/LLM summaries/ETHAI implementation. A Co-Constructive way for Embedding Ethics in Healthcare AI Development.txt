**Executive Summary**

This paper introduces the ETHAI (ETHics of AI) methodology, a novel multidisciplinary framework designed to bridge the persistent gap between abstract ethical principles and concrete technical implementation in artificial intelligence systems developed for healthcare applications. While existing theoretical frameworks are abundant, ETHAI addresses the practical disconnect through a co-constructive, iterative process characterized by four core principles: contextual awareness, practical application, cross-disciplinary collaboration, and iterative refinement. The methodology operates on the premise that AI ethics must be co-constructed, rather than merely translated, through continuous interaction among ethicists, technical developers, clinical researchers, and potential users, necessitating the formation of dedicated multidisciplinary working groups.

The implementation of the ETHAI methodology within the European Commission-funded COMFORTage project, which focuses on developing AI systems for monitoring patients with dementia and age-related frailty, demonstrates its practical utility. Using the EU Guidelines for Trustworthy AI as a foundational framework, the process established baseline ethical requirements that undergo continuous improvement and refinement across four interconnected phases that form a continuous cycle. The preliminary results show that the participatory risk assessment provided by the method successfully identified domain-specific considerations, such as the vulnerability of older adults, requiring the adaptation of generic ethical principles—confirming the inherent limitations of domain-agnostic guidelines. Furthermore, the semi-structured, flexible approach proved advantageous compared to rigid methodologies, demonstrating that meaningful ethics integration can occur even when introduced at the design phase instead of project conception, allowing ethical development and technical work to proceed simultaneously.

This co-design approach fosters greater appreciation of ethics considerations among technical partners and deeper understanding of implementation constraints among ethicists, effectively addressing cross-disciplinary communication challenges. By engaging diverse stakeholders in collaborative requirements development, the methodology increases ownership and facilitates deliberation to generate consensus around how system impacts should be defined and prioritized, establishing requirements such as implementing mechanisms to preserve human decision-making authority in crucial matters (Autonomy and Oversight). By documenting the successes and challenges encountered in a real-world scenario, this work contributes valuable empirical evidence to the field of applied AI ethics and design, where practical case studies remain scarce, while proposing future work focused on refining methods for operationalizing abstract concepts into measurable technical criteria.
