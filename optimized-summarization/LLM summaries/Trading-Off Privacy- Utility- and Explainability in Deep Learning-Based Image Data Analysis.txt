Executive Summary

This paper introduces a novel methodology designed to navigate the complex trade-offs among data privacy, decision explainability, and data utility within multi-party collaborative deep learning-based image data analysis. Recognizing that these measures have typically been addressed as separate problems, the proposed approach analytically relates them and provides a mechanism to tune the parameters of privacy-preserving and explainability techniques to achieve an optimal balance based on participant requirements. The research focuses on image data analysis problems, utilizing $(\epsilon, \delta)$-Differential Privacy and Autoencoders as privacy-preserving techniques, while the SmoothGrad mechanism is adopted to provide decision explainability. This methodology formalizes core elements of trustworthy AI and offers tools for designing customized image analytics systems that align with user specifications for safer and more explainable experiences.

The proposed methodology was validated through experiments involving three multi-class deep learning classifiers across three well-known image datasets: MNIST, FER, and CIFAR-10. The effectiveness of the approach was assessed by tracking a trade-off score defined as a function of Data Utility Loss, Privacy Gain, and Explainability Gain. Findings revealed that the initial utilization of a privacy mechanism, such as Differential Privacy, resulted in an immediate Utility Loss; however, subsequent increases in the Privacy Degree parameter did not significantly exacerbate this loss. Notably, the adoption of the Differential Privacy technique led to a more significant impact on Utility Loss in the facial expression recognition model (FER) compared to the digit classification or object recognition models. This differentiation is attributed to the fact that the Differential Privacy mechanism minimizes model memorization of potentially identifying face attributes, thus affecting the analysis accuracy more acutely in sensitive domains.

Experimental results indicate a relevant positive correlation between the trade-off score and the increase in both the privacy degree and explainability degree, primarily driven by improved privacy gain and explainability gain achieved as these parameters are incremented. For instance, tuning the DP noise value or the Explainability noise degree parameter leads to meaningful increases in the overall trade-off score. Despite this positive relationship, the overall trade-off increase is restricted by the initial decrease in data utility that occurs immediately upon the adoption of any privacy mechanism. The study concludes that the privacy and explainability degree parameters play a pivotal role in shaping the outcomes, underscoring that achieving an optimal trade-off is a non-trivial task requiring careful consideration and tailoring of parameter settings to specific, domain-dependent application requirements.
