Executive Summary

Edge-based computing has emerged as a powerful paradigm for Driver Monitoring Systems (DMS), allowing for localized, low-latency processing of sensitive real-time sensor data, including facial cues and vehicle dynamics. However, the decentralized nature of these systems raises significant privacy and security concerns, as traditional privacy-preserving techniques—such as data anonymization or standard differential privacy (DP)—often fail to maintain analysis accuracy necessary for real-time behavioral analytics, like identifying fatigue or distraction, while preventing data inference attacks. To address this critical gap, this research introduces an Adaptive Differential Privacy (ADP) mechanism designed specifically for edge-based DMS that provides dynamic noise injection based on the context and sensitivity of the data, thereby preserving personal information while maintaining the high fidelity needed for driver behavior analysis.

The proposed ADP mechanism employs a hybrid differential privacy strategy that integrates both Laplace and Gaussian noise techniques, selecting the appropriate mechanism based on the data’s sensitivity and the situational risk presented. This model utilizes a Dynamic Data Processing approach at the Distributor layer to achieve high generalizability and ensures system durability against inference attacks, with a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) being used to categorize driver states, such as eye and mouth states, for detecting drowsiness and distraction. Comparative experiments against standard Laplace and Gaussian DP models demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed system, reporting a remarkable 28.7% reduction in privacy leakage and 10-20% shorter latencies than existing differential privacy methods, while also achieving a 1.7% increase in utility (accuracy).

Overall, the ADP system presents a highly balanced approach between privacy, accuracy, and timeliness, achieving the lowest privacy leakage while maintaining a relatively high degree of utility and low latency compared to other current privacy-preserving mechanisms. This compromise is suitable for practical deployment scenarios requiring privacy-aware, real-time behavioral analytics. Future work stemming from this research could explore the integration of the adaptive mechanism into federated learning frameworks, enhance adaptivity using machine learning models that dynamically tune privacy parameters based on real-time feedback, and benchmark the approach against emerging privacy techniques, such as homomorphic encryption and secure multiparty computation, to further validate its applicability in edge and mobile computing environments.
