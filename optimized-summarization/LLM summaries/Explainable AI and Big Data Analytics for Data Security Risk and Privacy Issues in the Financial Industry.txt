Executive Summary

This research addresses the existing gaps in maintaining data security during processing using Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) and Big Data Analytics (BDA) technology within the financial industry, gaps which frequently lead to data breaches during information storage and processing. The exploitation and destruction of data while simultaneously ensuring the imperative of information security is resounding, particularly given the increasing reliance on complex algorithms in financial decision making that raises concerns about transparency and accountability. This necessity for secure systems extends to structured, unstructured, and semistructured data crucial for informed decision making without compromising accuracy. The study utilized databases including IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, ScienceDirect, and Google Scholar, conducting a comprehensive review of the literature published between January 2015 and December 2023, using specific search terms such as combinations of "explainable AI," "big data analytics," "financial security," "data privacy," and "risk assessment."

The financial industry leverages these analytical techniques across notable domains including customer segmentation, profitability analysis, credit assessment, payment default prediction, detection of fraudulent transactions, investment ranking, optimization of stock portfolios, cash management, and operational forecasting. Various methods have been proposed to explain black box models, ranging from model-agnostic approaches to model-specific techniques, with classification depending on the availability of existing and appropriate models for the data source. One specific mechanism described, Model A, is an Explainable Neural Network that combines the power of deep learning with interpretability, using a layered architecture where each neuron's decision-making process can be traced and explained. The challenge of protecting the mappers and data within certain frameworks revolves around ensuring that assigned tasks are executed securely without compromise, providing significance to the consideration of untrusted or malicious mappers.

The primary benefit prospect of this research project is to provide an understanding of the need for secure artificial intelligence and data analysis systems for the decision-making process in the finance industry, which is crucial as the progression will be marked by an exponential surge in data volumes. The best method to judge whether the XAI tool is reliable is with the help of a survey, and one approach highlighted offers a user-friendly advantage by eliminating the need for program coding, thus facilitating the processing of large datasets through enhanced security layers. The insights provided in this document are beneficial for all future research carried out in this field, as XAI also possesses the ability to predict future attacks.
