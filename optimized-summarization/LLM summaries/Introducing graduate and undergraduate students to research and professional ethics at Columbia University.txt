Executive Summary
A several-hour seminar, titled "Problems in Applied Physics," has been developed at Columbia University to introduce graduate and undergraduate students to the responsible conduct of research and the ethics of professionalism, addressing the gap where other less extensive training options, such as multi-day or multi-week workshops, are neither widely offered nor required. This program is built around a three-component structure where the discussion of relevant and related issues and their potential consequences is paramount. The instruction begins with a brief presentation of several recent egregious public examples of ethical lapses, including those related to plagiarism, data falsification, and gaming college rankings, while also integrating humorous examples from the public culture, such as the Tom Lehrer satire "Lobachevsky," to foster engagement.

The central instructional component involves an extensive, continually updated series of mini case scenarios developed by the author, with each case presented on a slide for detailed group discussion. This method allows a broad spectrum of key issues in ethics to be raised and explored, including recognition of the considerable gray area that exists in ethical decision-making, differentiating a legitimate difference in opinion from a clear transgression, and identifying the threshold for pursuing formal or casual complaints. This interactive approach sometimes involves dividing the larger group into smaller sections to tackle complex scenarios that commonly branch into several directions, thus illustrating the potential complexities of solutions; this process has been shown to engage and interest students more effectively than straightforward, dry lectures.

The overarching goal of this ethics training is to stimulate students to actively think about professional ethics, increase awareness of potential transgressions—which can sometimes reflect cultural issues or generational shifts—and cause some to worry if they consider performing an ethical lapse, based on the principle that the truth will ultimately come out. Although most situations described are uncommon, the seminar emphasizes that some are more common than commonly thought. Students who repeat the seminar over extended periods are continually exposed to new examples, ensuring the training remains current and effective in providing guidance in evaluating and handling ethical issues.
