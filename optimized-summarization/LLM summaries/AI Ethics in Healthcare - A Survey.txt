**Executive Summary**
This paper presents a comprehensive survey examining the intricate tapestry of AI ethics within the healthcare landscape, aiming to delineate multifaceted challenges and propose viable solutions for mitigation. A pivotal focus is bridging a conspicuous research gap concerning the collaboration between medical practitioners and AI developers, thereby ensuring AI systems align with the actual requirements of healthcare providers. The study addresses the moral conundrums engendered by AI’s lack of emotional intelligence, noting that the absence of human emotional comprehension has, in certain instances, led to grievous outcomes, necessitating an equilibrium where intelligent machines operate under prudent human oversight to balance precision and compassion.

The research emphasizes that the deployment of AI requires a contextually sensitive approach, devoid of a one-size-fits-all mentality, recognizing that AI’s global applicability necessitates contextual adjustments. The necessity for human intervention to mitigate incidents emerges as an ethical requirement, debunking the notion that AI can completely replace human oversight, as illustrated by a case study where a robotic procedure malfunctioned and the system failed to issue an error signal. Furthermore, bias within AI’s digital fabric amplifies the call for ethical governance, demanding recognition and elimination of discrimination to promote equity, particularly in light of historical instances of healthcare professionals disregarding the needs of people of color. Data protection practices are vital to adapt to evolving threats and maintain the trust and security of patient information.

Ethical and legal obligations for healthcare providers include obtaining informed consent for AI-driven interventions, complying with regulations such as GDPR or HIPAA for patient data privacy, and implementing continual monitoring and evaluation for assessment of AI systems. The paper contributes to the ongoing discourse surrounding the responsible integration of AI technologies in healthcare settings by dissecting the principles of fairness, accountability, and transparency, thereby paving the way for a cogent framework governing AI deployment. Addressing biases further requires diverse stakeholder involvement, including patients. While the authors acknowledge their focus on academic literature might inadvertently exclude other significant sources of information, the study underscores the imperative for ethical introspection to shape a future where AI and healthcare coalesce ethically, ultimately benefiting both patients and practitioners.
