**Executive Summary**

This paper introduces DIPALDA (DIstributed, Privacy-Aware Location Data Aggregation), a novel anonymization technique designed to mitigate the inherent privacy threats associated with aggregating sensitive personal location data, particularly when linked to private attributes like health information, such as in identifying COVID-19 outbreaks. Recognizing that analyzing such information requires strong protection against re-identification and location privacy attacks from honest-but-curious third parties, DIPALDA addresses the limitations of previous approaches like Differential Privacy (DP), whose privacy budget is often an abstract value difficult for non-experts to understand. To promote trust and acceptance, DIPALDA explicitly links its privacy guarantees to explainable parameters, which include $k$-anonymity, minimum cloaking area size, and maximum re-identification probability, thereby directly addressing potential attacks.

The DIPALDA framework is applied to hierarchically structured location data, exemplified initially using a Region Quadtree, which recursively divides space into four equal subareas until a maximum depth is reached, but is generally applicable to any data-independent hierarchical index with a predefined structure. The system ensures privacy by allowing each user to set individual privacy parameters, ensuring a user’s location is only encoded for hierarchy levels where their individual parameters align with the shared privacy parameters of the data structure, and ensuring the aggregated result is only released if the shared privacy parameters for that vertex are fulfilled. While the approach is initially simplified using a trusted coordinator, methods like Threshold FHE can be employed for distributed key generation to prevent any single party from accessing users' encryption keys.

Extensive experiments utilizing COVID-19 propagation data demonstrate that DIPALDA achieves a suitable balance between utility, privacy, and explainability. Using the ground truth (Quadtrees constructed without privacy constraints) as a baseline, the evaluation found that the Relative Error, which serves as a lower bound for utility loss, remained below 4% in non-rural areas (and below 18% in rural areas) for a comparatively strict re-identification likelihood of $p \ge 10\%$. This focus on explainable privacy guarantees, which directly show protection from re-identification and location privacy attacks, is crucial for real-world feasibility. DIPALDA can therefore be leveraged for other applications relying on spatial hierarchies, such as privately analyzing employment status using census or administrative boundaries, or analyzing sales activities based on custom internal company hierarchies.
