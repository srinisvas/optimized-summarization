**Executive Summary**

The proliferation of AI-infused technologies continues to raise significant ethical concerns related to issues such as fairness, bias, privacy, discrimination, and sustainability, necessitating robust preparation for future graduates. This work-in-progress paper investigates whether undergraduates in Australian Computer Science and IT programs are being adequately equipped to navigate these profound impacts on individuals and societies. The importance of macro-ethical concepts, such as societal and environmental well-being, is growing as Computer Science and IT are increasingly implicated in political, economic, and social life, demanding that curricula keep pace with these changing priorities and the expansion of professional bodies' codes of ethics.

The research explored the ethics curricula of 12 Australian universities' Computer Science courses, discovering significant variation in course content. Preliminary findings indicate an emphasis on micro-ethical concepts, with most courses focusing on professional issues, compliance with industry codes and standards, and ensuring traditional values like loyalty, obedience, and competence in job performance. Conversely, philosophical and macro-ethical aspects are neglected, resulting in less focus on actively using technology to promote social good, fairness, inclusivity, and social justice. Translating broad ethical principles—such as those defining human agency, oversight, and non-discrimination—into policy and practice remains a challenge, and the study observed that only two universities explicitly teach ethics philosophy as part of their course offerings.

The identified lack of explicit macro-ethical agendas invites further work on the efficacy of delivery modes and the adequacy of curricular offerings. The broader project aims to address this gap by surveying the current status of ethics across Australian engineering universities with a view toward creating recommendations for content, pedagogies, and effective assessment of ethics learning. This involves supplementing the initial website-based research with an in-depth study of detailed course materials and interviews with relevant academics. The ultimate goal is to develop a comprehensive understanding of how macro-ethics may be effectively incorporated into CS and IT education, recognizing this as an ongoing challenge given the pace of technological and sociological change in this critical domain.
