**Executive Summary**

This paper introduces a novel scheme for protecting the privacy of AI model training data, specifically designed for distributed and data fusion environments, utilizing Local Differential Privacy (LDP) to address the inherent conflict between robust data security and model utility. The necessity of this research stems from the reliance of AI systems on large datasets containing sensitive personal information, requiring protection beyond traditional methods like encryption or anonymization, which often degrade model performance. While existing privacy work in data fusion frequently focuses on Secure Multi-Party Computation or Homomorphic Encryption, the proposed scheme leverages the local application of differential privacy to ensure noise is added directly to the data at the point of collection, guaranteeing privacy protection before data transmission, thereby excelling in both scalability and computational efficiency.

The methodology employs a dynamic noise adjustment mechanism across two critical phases of the AI pipeline. During the data preparation phase, the scheme involves standardization—scaling features to a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one—along with handling missing values and ensuring format consistency, where the privacy budget ($\epsilon$) and noise distribution parameters are initially chosen. Subsequently, during the iterative training process, dynamic noise, generated as Laplace noise (defined by $p(z) = \frac{1}{2b} \exp(-\frac{|z|}{b})$), is added to both the data and the model gradients. The model is iteratively updated using this noisy gradient until convergence, and performance is evaluated after a set number of iterations to determine if the current level of privacy and noise is appropriate, ensuring the dynamic adjustment optimally balances protection strength with data utility.

The proposed LDP-based scheme successfully offers robust privacy guarantees and maintains high model accuracy, particularly demonstrating that for moderate values of $\epsilon$, strong privacy guarantees are achieved without significant utility loss. The research validates that this approach exhibits strong scalability and computational efficiency, significantly reducing overhead compared to centralized differential privacy techniques. The primary contribution is a robust, privacy-preserving AI model training method well-suited for real-world applications involving sensitive data from multiple sources, with future work planned to extend the method to more complex data fusion scenarios and explore adaptive privacy mechanisms.
