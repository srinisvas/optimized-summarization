Executive Summary

The proliferation of Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems and their widespread use in various aspects of life and society have instigated moral problems, leading to the emergence of AI Ethics—an applied field consisting of scientific research and practical implementation through numerous ethical documents such as declarations, recommendations, ethical codes, and ethical standards. This analysis of existing ethical documents develops a typology based on differences in their statuses, sources of origin, and moral agency, recognizing that some are proactive codes proposed by professional communities and specialists. A consistent finding across these texts is the prioritization of human safety and well-being, stipulating that the interaction or actions of AI agents remain outside the boundaries of ethics unless they pose a harm or danger to people or humanity as a whole. This focus ensures norms are valid across all types of AI, and the general abstractness of the wording indicates these documents were compiled primarily for human stakeholders.

The study identifies specific ethical issues arising in AI technologies, paying particular attention to the concepts of transparency, privacy, and trust. While many of the principles discussed are common to all technologies, these three are uniquely specific in the context of AI use, especially considering the large amount of personal data processed by smart devices. However, the analysis reveals significant points of discrepancy and uncertainty in the understanding and interpretation of these terms across various documents; for instance, the concept of "good" remains quite vague, hindering the development of comparable strategies for its increase. Examples, such as the formulation of user agreements that favor companies by allowing the manipulation of received data, or historical aircraft software failures due to incorrect information perception, underscore the necessity for clearer normative and ethical regulation.

As a main conclusion, the research posits that most existing ethical documents have successfully completed the initial stage of identifying the main issues and concepts within the field of AI ethics, although interpretations remain controversial. The significant drawback noted across all analyzed documents, however, is the insufficient development of the practical component. There is a universal lack of elaboration on the practical application of stated principles, which is deemed necessary to form a concrete basis for decision-making aimed at preventing and resolving the moral problems arising from AI design and use. The critical task moving forward is the development of consolidated practical recommendations to operationalize these principles and improve the level of trust among ordinary users regarding the application of these technologies in everyday life.
