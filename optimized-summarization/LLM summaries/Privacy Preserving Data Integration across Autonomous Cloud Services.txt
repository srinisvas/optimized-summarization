Executive Summary

This paper addresses the critical privacy challenges inherent in data sharing and integration across autonomous cloud environments, proposing a service-oriented, privacy-preserving model to facilitate secure data aggregation. The research identifies a significant gap wherein existing data integration solutions, typically implemented as centralized data warehouses, fail to enforce adequate local data protection. The proposed model allows for the execution of complex data sharing services, such as those found in collaborative healthcare information systems managing synthetic medical information of over 30,000 patients, without revealing extraneous or protected information to any of the involved services, operating within a designated honest-but-curious environment.

The solution relies on two key principles designed to maintain local autonomy and privacy integrity: involved data services enforce their local privacy policies by applying their own access control models and data anonymization algorithms. Crucially, the system utilizes the Order Preserving Encryption Scheme (OPES) to enable the composition execution engine to handle only anonymized data. This mechanism allows the engine to link the anonymized information of the same data subject across different services using encrypted identifier attributes, facilitating equality queries on encrypted data. Furthermore, the model incorporates a $k$-protection requirement, which mandates that when a service is invoked, it cannot precisely determine its specific input value between $k$ possible input values, thereby preventing disclosure risks, such as determining specific patients who tested positive for HIV when a low protection factor is used.

The composition execution engine manages edge constraints to filter relayed tuples and enforce the required protection factor. The methodology illustrates how services can be composed to answer complex research questions, such as retrieving ABC content for obtained HIV medications. Performance evaluation demonstrated the technical feasibility of the approach, showing that the average execution time with privacy measures implemented was at most three orders of magnitude greater than the time without privacy protections when the protection factor $K_i$ was set to four, affirming the model’s viability for robust and privacy-compliant data integration across autonomous cloud services.
