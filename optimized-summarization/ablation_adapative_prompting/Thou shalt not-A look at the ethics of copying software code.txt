**Executive Summary**

This paper explores the ethical landscape of copying computer software code, examining how software engineers balance intellectual property rights with the ethical principles outlined by the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) and the IEEE-CS. The study employs a descriptive survey approach to gather data on software engineers' practices and attitudes towards copying software code. The research focuses on three key areas: copyright, patent, and trade secret protections, each with distinct implications for software engineers' ethical behavior.

The survey results indicate a strong ethical commitment among software engineers, with a majority professing to comply with ACM/IEEE-CS ethical standards. However, the analysis reveals a nuanced landscape. For instance, 40% of respondents indicated they might use software obtained or retained illegally or unethically, suggesting a growing divide on the ethics of copying software code without proper authorization. This finding is further complicated by the third survey question, which showed a polarized response regarding the maintenance of employer trade secrets, with 31% of respondents not keeping such information private despite awareness of work practices for protection.

The fourth and fifth survey questions addressed the ethical implications of taking undue credit or making false claims about software characteristics. The results showed that 63% and 83% of respondents, respectively, accurately stated software characteristics and fully credited others' work, indicating that while ethical lapses may exist, they are not pervasive.

The study concludes that while bright line ethical rules may be too stringent, flexible codes of ethics appear effective in guiding software engineers' behavior. The findings underscore the importance of ongoing education and awareness about intellectual property laws, particularly regarding copyright, patent, and trade secret protections, to ensure ethical compliance in the copying of software code. Future research could explore the development of more user-friendly survey tools and the expansion of ethical guidelines to address emerging challenges in software engineering.
