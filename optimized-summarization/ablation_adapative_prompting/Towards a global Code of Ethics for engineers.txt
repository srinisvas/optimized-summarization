**Executive Summary**

This paper examines the compatibility of the IEEE Code of Ethics with the codes of ethics of 32 international professional engineering societies across Africa, Asia, Europe, and Latin America. The primary objective is to identify common articles that can be incorporated into a global code of ethics for engineers, ensuring alignment with the IEEE Code of Ethics while accommodating cultural and sociopolitical differences.

The IEEE Code of Ethics, which emphasizes responsibility, honesty, fairness, and integrity, is found to be partially or fully adopted by 28 out of the 32 international societies, with four countries adopting it in its entirety. Key findings reveal that articles related to public safety, conflict of interest, and ethical conduct are widely embraced, while others, such as those addressing cultural and societal responsibilities, are less consistently adopted.

Notably, the IEEE Code of Ethics Article 8, which mandates fair treatment of all persons regardless of factors like race, religion, gender, or nationality, is embraced by 12 countries (37.5% of the total), highlighting the need for cultural sensitivity in a global code. Similarly, Article 7, focusing on acknowledging and correcting errors, is adopted by 14 countries (44% of the total), indicating a strong emphasis on ethical conduct and accountability.

The research identifies 15 articles from international codes that are not included in the IEEE Code of Ethics but are adopted by at least four countries. These include articles related to cultural adaptation, fair competition, and ethical conduct in private practice. The analysis underscores the importance of integrating these articles into a global code to ensure inclusivity and alignment with diverse professional and societal contexts.

The paper concludes that while a unified global code of ethics for engineers is feasible, it must be crafted with careful consideration of cultural and sociopolitical differences. By incorporating widely accepted articles from international codes and addressing the unique needs of various engineering societies, a comprehensive and globally applicable code can be developed, enhancing the ethical standards and professional integrity of engineers worldwide.
