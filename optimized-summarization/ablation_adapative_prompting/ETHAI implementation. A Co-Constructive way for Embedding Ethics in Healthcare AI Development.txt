**Executive Summary**

This paper introduces the ETHAI (Ethics of AI) methodology, a novel approach to integrating ethical considerations into artificial intelligence systems designed for healthcare applications. The ETHAI framework is designed to bridge the gap between abstract ethical principles and practical implementation by fostering interdisciplinary collaboration among ethicists, technical developers, clinical experts, and potential users. The methodology is characterized by four key principles: contextual awareness, practical application, cross-disciplinary collaboration, and iterative refinement.

The ETHAI approach was implemented within the COMFORTage project, a European Commission-funded initiative aimed at developing AI systems for monitoring and supporting patients with dementia and age-related frailty. The project involved 39 organizations with diverse expertise, including technical partners, university researchers, clinical practitioners, computer scientists, social scientists, and humanists. The ETHAI methodology was applied through a co-constructive process that involved multidisciplinary working groups, ensuring that ethical considerations were integrated into the system's design and development from the earliest stages.

Initial findings from the ETHAI implementation within COMFORTage highlight the importance of contextual awareness and the need for practical application of ethical principles. The methodology facilitated a comprehensive assessment of AI risks in healthcare, focusing on high-level requirements derived from the EU Guidelines for Trustworthy AI. These requirements were further unpacked into specific sub-requirements and specifications, which were refined through iterative collaboration between ethicists and technical developers.

The ETHAI approach demonstrated significant potential in addressing cross-disciplinary communication challenges and ensuring that ethical considerations are integrated into the development process. The methodology's semi-structured nature allowed for a more flexible and adaptive approach compared to rigid frameworks, facilitating better engagement from stakeholders with diverse perspectives. The integration of domain-specific ethical considerations, such as those related to dementia care and older adults' vulnerability, was crucial in ensuring that the AI system's design and implementation effectively addressed the unique challenges of healthcare.

The ETHAI methodology provides valuable insights into the practical implementation of AI ethics in healthcare, offering a framework for bridging the gap between theoretical ethical principles and concrete technical implementation. Future work should focus on refining methods for operationalizing abstract ethical concepts into measurable technical criteria, expanding participatory risk assessment across diverse stakeholder groups, and developing more sophisticated tools to support interdisciplinary communication. By documenting both the successes and challenges encountered in implementing ETHAI within a real-world healthcare AI development project, this paper contributes to the growing body of practical experience in ethical AI development.
