**Executive Summary**

This paper addresses the ethical challenges posed by the application of artificial intelligence (AI), particularly in balancing competing values such as fairness, transparency, and accountability. The authors argue that traditional ethical frameworks, such as Kantian ethics or utilitarianism, are insufficient for resolving the conflicts that arise when implementing intelligent systems. These conflicts often lead to an infinite regress of higher-level principles, making theoretical discussions unhelpful for practical problem-solving.

The paper proposes a novel approach by focusing on specific criteria for implementing ethical values in AI. This shift from abstract principles to concrete criteria aims to provide a clearer framework for public debates about technological advancements. The authors emphasize the need to evaluate AI implementations based on their ability to enable human intervention, rather than making further actions and interventions impossible. This evaluation should guide the ethical considerations of AI, ensuring that it aligns with societal values and promotes responsible innovation.

In conclusion, the paper advocates for a pragmatic approach to ethics in AI, prioritizing the development of clear, actionable criteria for ethical implementation. By moving away from abstract principles and towards specific, measurable guidelines, the work seeks to enhance the ethical discourse surrounding AI, fostering a more informed and responsible development of intelligent technologies.
