**Executive Summary**

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) tools, such as ChatGPT, into university education has become increasingly prevalent, driven by the need for interactive learning and enhanced student engagement. However, this trend raises significant ethical concerns that require careful consideration. This study reviews the existing literature on the ethics of AI in higher education, focusing on academic integrity, misleading AI outputs, education automation, data privacy, and the potential negative impact on fundamental human rights.

Ethical challenges in AI integration in education are multifaceted. Academic integrity is a primary concern, as the use of AI tools like ChatGPT can lead to issues such as plagiarism and misrepresentation of student work. Lee (2021) and Garrett et al. (2020) emphasize the importance of ethical AI use, particularly in writing and teaching processes. Foltynek et al. (2023) highlight the potential misalignment of AI tools with academic integrity, necessitating guidance for both students and academics. The ethical implications of AI in education extend beyond academic integrity, with concerns about misleading AI outputs and the potential for AI to automate educational processes, potentially displacing educators and diminishing the role of human instructors.

Data privacy is another critical ethical consideration, as the use of AI in education involves the collection and processing of sensitive student data. The literature underscores the need for robust data privacy measures to protect student information and ensure compliance with regulations such as GDPR. Additionally, the integration of AI in education raises questions about fundamental human rights, particularly the potential for AI to infringe upon these rights. Nguyen et al. (2023) emphasize the importance of careful consideration and ethical oversight in the deployment of AI technologies in educational settings.

To address these ethical concerns, the study recommends the development and dissemination of clear ethical guidelines for the use of AI in academic settings. Establishing robust oversight mechanisms to monitor and evaluate the ethical implications of AI applications in education is crucial. Additionally, training programs for both educators and students to enhance awareness of the ethical considerations associated with AI are essential. Emphasizing the importance of data privacy in the context of AI applications is necessary, and promoting the integration of AI into teaching practices while ensuring that educators possess not only technical skills but also pedagogical and ethical knowledge is vital. By addressing these ethical dimensions, stakeholders in the field of higher education can ensure that AI integration is conducted responsibly and in a manner that upholds the integrity and well-being of students and educators alike.
