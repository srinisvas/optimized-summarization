**Executive Summary**

The paper explores the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI) ethics and data privacy compliance, emphasizing the need for a balanced approach to ensure both the utility of AI systems and the protection of citizens' rights. The introduction highlights the transformative impact of AI across various sectors, including health, education, transportation, and e-commerce, while also raising ethical concerns such as misinformation, privacy erosion, and the erosion of legitimacy in AI-driven systems.

The analysis delves into the European Union's (EU) efforts to address AI ethics, noting the strategic advantage of the EU in regulating AI, which is framed within the context of strategic autonomy and sovereignty. The EU's commitment to ethical AI is underscored by its position as a global leader in developing safe, reliable, and ethical AI. In contrast, the United States (US) has a strong AI industry with a focus on governance, including ethics, and the promotion of democratic values such as privacy, freedom, and civil rights.

The paper examines the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) as a key framework for data privacy in the EU. GDPR emphasizes the importance of transparency, data minimization, and individual rights, such as access to personal data, correction of inaccuracies, and the right to be forgotten. However, the paper also points out the limitations of GDPR in addressing AI-specific issues, such as the lack of clear regulations for AI-driven data processing and the absence of a sanctioning mechanism.

The core of the paper revolves around the ethical implications of AI in digital media systems, highlighting the risks of AI systems, such as the potential for misinformation and the erosion of human rights. The authors argue that the ethical and non-ethical aspects of AI are often intertwined, making it challenging to separate the two. They propose a framework for ethical AI development, which includes transparency in algorithmic processes, accountability for AI decisions, and the need for legal frameworks that can adapt to the evolving nature of AI.

The paper concludes by advocating for a collaborative approach to AI regulation, emphasizing the importance of involving AI developers, universities, and research institutions in the development of ethical AI standards. The GDPR serves as a model for such a framework, offering a starting point for a universally accepted legislative approach to AI development and data privacy compliance. The work underscores the necessity of balancing AI's utility with ethical considerations to ensure that AI systems enhance societal benefits while respecting human rights and privacy.
