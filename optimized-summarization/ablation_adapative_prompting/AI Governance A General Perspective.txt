**Executive Summary**

The paper addresses the urgent topic of artificial intelligence (AI) governance, emphasizing the risks and challenges posed by its widespread use in various sectors. It highlights the potential infringement of privacy rights, discrimination, job displacement, security concerns, and ethical issues associated with AI. The paper discusses the importance of establishing AI governance frameworks to ensure ethical and responsible use of AI technologies.

Key principles of AI governance identified include accountability, transparency, fairness, data privacy, ethical use, inclusivity, sustainability, adaptability, collaboration, and human oversight. The paper outlines various international initiatives and principles that have been proposed to guide AI governance, such as the Shanghai Declaration, IMF Framework, UNESCO's AI Ethics Recommendation, OECD AI Principles, and the European AI Act.

The paper also introduces the concept of responsible AI institutions, emphasizing the need for cooperation among governments, the private sector, and civil society to establish and implement effective AI governance frameworks. It suggests that these frameworks should be adaptable to evolving technological changes and risks, and should prioritize human-centered design, ethical considerations, and sustainability.

The paper concludes by advocating for international cooperation, particularly under the auspices of the United Nations, to develop comprehensive AI governance frameworks. It recognizes the European Union's efforts in enacting AI legislation and encourages further research into AI governance frameworks, particularly in areas such as privacy protection, transparency, and sustainability. The paper aims to provide a comprehensive perspective on AI governance, highlighting both the opportunities and challenges associated with its development and use.
