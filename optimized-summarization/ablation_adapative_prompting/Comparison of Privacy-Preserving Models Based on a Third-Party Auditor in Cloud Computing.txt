**Executive Summary**

This paper compares privacy-preserving models in cloud computing based on a third-party auditor (TPA). It explores the vulnerabilities of cloud computing environments, such as lack of trust, loss of control, and various types of threats including collusion, intoxication, and discrimination. The study introduces a novel privacy-preserving model, Lightweight Privacy-Preserving Public Auditing (LAPP), which aims to balance security and data utility in cloud environments.

The authors discuss the role of a TPA in cloud computing, emphasizing its ability to audit data and ensure privacy. They compare the LAPP model with existing privacy-preserving models like SPS, PPA, PPPAS, and SEPPPA, highlighting their strengths and weaknesses. The simulations conducted using the Greencloud platform demonstrate that LAPP requires less processing time than its counterparts, especially when dealing with large volumes of data.

Key findings indicate that LAPP significantly reduces computational overhead and improves query response time compared to traditional privacy-preserving models. The paper concludes that while adopting a TPA-based solution can enhance security, it also introduces trust concerns and additional overhead. Future research is recommended to focus on developing lightweight, secure, and efficient privacy-preserving models that can alleviate the trust gap in cloud computing.
