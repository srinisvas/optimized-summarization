**Executive Summary**

This paper provides an in-depth analysis of privacy-preserving schemes for industrial Internet of Things (IoT) systems, addressing the critical challenges of data security and privacy in the context of Industry 4.0. The authors explore various architectural paradigms, including cloud-based and edge-based systems, and evaluate the effectiveness of different privacy protection strategies.

Industrial IoT systems, which integrate intelligent technologies into manufacturing processes, generate vast amounts of data that are vulnerable to privacy breaches. The paper identifies key privacy issues in cloud-based and edge-based architectures, such as data leakage, location privacy, identity privacy, and query privacy. It also discusses the computational complexity and privacy performance of existing solutions, such as data perturbation, encryption, and anonymization techniques.

The authors propose a comprehensive framework for addressing privacy challenges in industrial IoT, emphasizing the integration of quantum-resistant differential privacy (QRDP) to enhance security while maintaining data utility. QRDP is designed to scale efficiently in high-velocity, decentralized IoT networks, balancing privacy and utility effectively. The paper concludes by highlighting the importance of adopting QRDP protocols for future decentralized systems, ensuring regulatory compliance without compromising data utility, and suggesting avenues for further research in hardware integration and real-world quantum attack simulations.
