**Executive Summary**

This paper addresses two critical challenges in the ethical development of AI technology: the need for a systematic overview of relevant values and the necessity of an indicator system to assess the impact of design features on these values. The authors propose a comprehensive approach to these challenges, particularly in the context of AI-assisted rescue robots.

First, the paper systematically compares prominent AI ethics guidelines and recent proposals for AI regulation to identify shared values and principles. These values are derived from the principles of justice, well-being, and understanding, which are considered morally desirable and instrumentally valuable for achieving other desirable states. The authors argue that basing AI development on shared values is crucial in liberal democracies, where moral pluralism is common. This approach ensures that AI technology aligns with broader societal values, fostering trust and accountability.

Second, the paper introduces an indicator system for the ethical development of AI-assisted rescue robots, complementing the VCIO model with the addition of principles. This system is designed to measure the degree to which AI technology meets the relevant criteria. The indicator system is grounded in the VCIO model, which is hierarchically composed of values, criteria, indicators, and observables. By incorporating principles, the system becomes more nuanced and comprehensive, allowing for a more detailed assessment of ethical implications.

The indicator system is applied to the specific case of AI-assisted rescue robots, which are intended to support hazardous substance reconnaissance. The authors propose a hierarchical structure that includes values, principles, criteria, indicators, and observables. For instance, the value of understanding is linked to the principle of explainability, which in turn is linked to the criterion of transparency in uncertainties. An observable indicator in this context might be the visibility of uncertainties in the AI detection process for the robot's operator.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the importance of integrating ethical considerations into the development of AI-assisted rescue robots. By providing a structured framework for assessing the ethical impact of design features, the proposed indicator system offers a practical tool for technical developers and end-users. This framework ensures that AI technology is developed in a way that respects shared values and principles, thereby enhancing trust and accountability in AI-assisted rescue applications. Future work will focus on refining the framework and validating its effectiveness in real-world scenarios.
