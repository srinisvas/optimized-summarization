**Executive Summary**

The paper addresses the ethical challenges in Biomedical Engineering (BME) education, emphasizing the need for integrating ethics into the curriculum. It outlines a comprehensive Ethics program of study for BME students, covering fundamental ethics, research ethics, professional ethics, and social ethics. The program is designed to prepare students to handle the ethical implications of their work, such as conflicts of interest, public health, and patient-physician relationships. The authors argue that ethics should be a core component of BME education, not just an add-on, to ensure graduates are equipped to perform their duties under strong moral standards.

The program includes modules on Meta-ethics, Descriptive and Comparative Ethics, Normative Ethics, and the analysis of ethical theories like Utilitarianism, value theory, and divine command. Research ethics are discussed, including the role of ethical review boards, conflicts of interest, and scientific misconduct. Professional ethics cover engineering ethics, medical ethics, and the ethical implications of designing and manufacturing medical devices. Social ethics integrate ethical, social, and legal issues, emphasizing community concerns and the impact of engineering solutions on a global context.

The paper also provides case studies to illustrate ethical dilemmas in BME, such as the Björk-Shiley heart valve and the Therac-25 medical linear accelerator. These cases highlight the importance of ethical considerations in engineering practice and the consequences of neglecting them. The authors advocate for the use of various teaching and learning methodologies, including group discussions, case studies, and role-playing, to foster critical thinking and ethical decision-making among BME students. They conclude that integrating ethics into the BME curriculum is essential for producing professionals who can navigate the complex ethical landscape of their field.
