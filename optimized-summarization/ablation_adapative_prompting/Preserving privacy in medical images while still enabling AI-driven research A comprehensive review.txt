**Executive Summary**

The application of AI in medical image processing requires access to large datasets containing protected health information (PHI). Preserving individuals' privacy is crucial, as mandated by regulations like HIPAA or GDPR. However, anonymizing medical images is challenging due to the potential for re-identification. This paper provides an overview of techniques and tools used for de-identification or anonymization of unstructured medical data, particularly medical images, while addressing the limitations and challenges faced by researchers in this area.

A comprehensive review of the state-of-the-art reveals that most studies focus on de-identification rather than anonymization, with half of the relevant papers employing the RSNA CTP pipeline. This solution is adaptable to different imaging modalities and can be personalized according to specific requirements. Other approaches include distorting facial features using a de-facer program or generating synthetic MRIs using Generative Adversarial Networks. Challenges include the complexity of image modalities, varying DICOM headers, and the need for standardized de-identification processes across different authorities (GDPR, HIPAA, etc.).

Future directions include improving de-identified dataset management, optimizing de-identification with machine learning OCR methods, and enhancing dataset metadata to increase utility. The review underscores the importance of addressing input data quality and standardizing de-identification processes across different vendors or data owners to ensure robust privacy preservation while maintaining the utility of medical image datasets for AI-driven research.
