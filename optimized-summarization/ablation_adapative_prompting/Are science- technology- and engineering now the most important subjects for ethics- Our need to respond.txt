**Executive Summary**

Science, technology, and engineering (STE) have dramatically expanded human power, enabling capabilities that were previously unimaginable. This unprecedented access to power necessitates a reevaluation of ethics, as it directly impacts the pursuit of human excellence and flourishing. The qualitative changes in human action and power, as highlighted by philosophers like Hans Jonas, indicate that ethics must adapt to address the new dimensions of human influence.

The field of ethics, particularly in relation to STE, is underdeveloped compared to other disciplines. STE ethics is crucial for addressing the ethical implications of these powerful technologies, which have the potential to both benefit and harm humanity. The ethical challenges posed by STE are significant, ranging from environmental degradation to existential risks, necessitating a more robust and integrated approach to ethics.

STE professionals have a unique responsibility to ensure that their creations are used ethically and responsibly. This responsibility extends beyond individual actions to the broader societal context, where their work can have far-reaching consequences. Professionalization of STE practitioners, including licensing and regulatory measures, is essential to ensure accountability and prevent misuse of technology.

Governance at the grand scale required by STE is also necessary. Current institutional structures may not adequately address the ethical and governance challenges posed by these technologies. Public participation and informed consent are vital components of this governance, ensuring that the public is engaged and informed in the decision-making processes.

In conclusion, science, technology, and engineering are now the most important subjects for ethics to study. STE professionals must take on a proactive role in ethical considerations, ensuring that their innovations are guided by a commitment to the health, safety, and welfare of humanity. This requires a comprehensive approach that includes education, professionalization, and governance, to effectively manage the power and responsibilities that come with these technologies.
