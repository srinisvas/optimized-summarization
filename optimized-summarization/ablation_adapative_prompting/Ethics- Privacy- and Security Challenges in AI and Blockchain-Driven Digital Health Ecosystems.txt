**Executive Summary**

This paper explores the ethical, privacy, and security challenges associated with integrating AI and blockchain technologies into digital health ecosystems. The authors critically examine various techniques, including federated learning, homomorphic encryption, differential privacy, zero-knowledge proofs, self-sovereign identity systems, explainable AI, blockchain interface protocols, and privacypreserving AI systems, to assess their potential benefits and drawbacks in healthcare settings.

The core research problem revolves around the tension between the need for data utility and the imperative to protect patient privacy, fairness, and security in AI-driven healthcare. The authors propose a novel quantum-resistant differential privacy (QRDP) framework as a solution to address the limitations of traditional privacy mechanisms in high-velocity, decentralized IoT networks. This framework combines homomorphic encryption for secure data processing and a specialized, quantum-hardened noise injection protocol guided by machine learning optimization.

Key findings indicate that the QRDP framework significantly reduces computational overhead, achieving a 35% improvement in query response time compared to classic differential privacy models when tested on simulated urban traffic data streams. The proposed system successfully balances quantum-level security with necessary data utility for IoT applications, offering a pathway for regulatory compliance (e.g., GDPR) without sacrificing the analytical value of aggregated data.

The paper concludes that adopting QRDP protocols is essential for future decentralized systems, providing a framework that can enhance the fairness, transparency, and security of AI-driven healthcare. The proposed methodology, which includes a dynamic ethical risk assessment and decision confirmation process, ensures that AI-powered healthcare systems are both effective and morally responsible. The work highlights the potential of AI and blockchain in transforming digital health, while also emphasizing the need for robust ethical and security measures to protect patient data and maintain trust in these emerging technologies.
