**Executive Summary**

This paper introduces a stakeholder-oriented taxonomical approach to address the security and privacy challenges in cloud computing (CC). The proposed taxonomy categorizes stakeholders based on their interaction with CC models, enabling a comprehensive understanding of the security concerns associated with different deployment and service models. By structuring the taxonomy into five levels, the authors aim to facilitate extensions and upgrades in the future. The first level focuses on stakeholders, the second on deployment models, the third on service models, and the fourth on potential security and privacy issues. The fifth level provides a list of suggested security and privacy attributes.

The taxonomy is grounded in the NIST characteristics of cloud computing, ensuring alignment with industry standards. It is designed to be both taxonomical, providing a clear structure for different use cases, and stakeholder-oriented, focusing on the diverse interactions of stakeholders with CC models. The approach aims to help stakeholders identify and address specific security and privacy issues relevant to their roles and interactions with the cloud.

Key contributions of the paper include the identification of security and privacy issues for various CC stakeholders, the recommendation of associated attributes, and the demonstration of the taxonomy's ability to balance security and data utility. The authors emphasize the importance of this approach in promoting security and privacy among stakeholders and in addressing regulatory compliance challenges in diverse industries and locations.

Future work will focus on validating the framework against real-world scenarios and exploring hardware-level integration to enhance the practical applicability of the proposed taxonomy. The work concludes by highlighting the potential of the proposed approach to improve the security and privacy landscape in cloud computing, offering a pathway for regulatory compliance without compromising the analytical value of aggregated data.
