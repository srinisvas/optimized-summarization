**Executive Summary**

This paper examines the ethical implications of corporations boycotting in response to the proliferation of hate speech on social media platforms, particularly focusing on Facebook. The authors argue that the failure of these platforms to adequately address hate speech constitutes a form of injustice that warrants corporate action. They propose that boycotting by corporations is a morally permissible and potentially obligatory response, challenging the traditional view that individual boycotts are the primary form of political expression. The paper suggests that corporations have a unique ethical obligation to act in response to systemic injustices, especially when these injustices are facilitated by their business practices. However, it also highlights the potential risks of corporate boycotts, such as their impact on democratic deliberation and equal participation in political discourse. The authors propose preliminary principles for the ethics of corporate boycotts, linking these principles to different paradigms of corporate social responsibility (CSR). They conclude that corporate boycotts can be a morally justified form of activism, but they also underscore the need for careful consideration of the broader implications for democratic processes. The paper contributes to ongoing debates about the ethical responsibilities of corporations, particularly in the context of social media platforms and hate speech.
