**Executive Summary**

This paper argues that science, technology, and engineering (STE) have become the most critical subjects for ethics to study, necessitating a reevaluation of human behavior in light of the enhanced power these fields have brought about. The authors emphasize that ethics must adapt to the new reality where human actions are significantly amplified by scientific and technological advancements. This shift is not only a challenge for ethics but also for STE professionals, who are urged to integrate ethical considerations into their work to ensure that their creations are used for the betterment of society and not for harm.

The paper highlights the dual responsibility of STE professionals: they must be both ethical and professionalized. The integration of ethics into STE curricula is proposed as a crucial step forward. This is supported by the observation that STE professionals often face challenges in balancing their roles as creators and enforcers of ethical standards, particularly when confronted with powerful forces that may undermine their ability to act ethically. The authors advocate for a more cohesive approach, where STE professionals are not merely components of a larger system but are recognized as entities with a significant impact on societal welfare.

The paper also touches on the ethical implications of environmental changes, such as the alteration of atmospheric compositions and resulting climate shifts, which are exacerbated by human activities. It suggests that ethical considerations should guide the development and deployment of STE technologies, ensuring that they are used responsibly and with consideration for long-term environmental and societal impacts. The authors argue that this proactive approach is essential for addressing the complex ethical challenges posed by STE, and that it is imperative for STE professionals to be well-versed in ethical frameworks to effectively navigate these issues.

In conclusion, the paper calls for a renewed focus on ethics within STE, advocating for mandatory ethics courses for all STE students and a greater emphasis on ethical responsibility among STE professionals. By doing so, it aims to foster a culture of ethical awareness and action, ensuring that the rapid advancements in STE are harnessed for the benefit of humanity. The paper concludes that this shift is not only necessary but also feasible, offering a roadmap for integrating ethics into the core of STE education and practice.
