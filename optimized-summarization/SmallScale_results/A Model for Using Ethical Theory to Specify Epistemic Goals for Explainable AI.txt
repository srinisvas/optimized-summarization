**Executive Summary**

This paper addresses the challenge of ensuring moral accountability for artificial intelligence (AI) as it becomes increasingly complex and capable of complex behaviors. The authors argue that AI, especially those designed for explainable AI (XAI), must meet certain normative requirements to be held accountable for their actions. The primary concern is the availability of information about the AI's decision-making processes, which is crucial for assigning responsibility and preventing harm.

The authors propose that AI must be capable of providing sufficient epistemic access to their inner workings, akin to how humans can provide explanations for their actions. This is crucial because, as AI systems become more autonomous and complex, they may exhibit behaviors that are morally troubling, similar to those of humans. The authors draw on Nomy Arpaly's account of moral-worthiness, which distinguishes between praise-worthy and blameworthy actions, to argue that AI must meet specific conditions to be held accountable.

The paper introduces the concept of XAI as a means to achieve this goal. XAI aims to provide transparent and understandable explanations for AI decisions, which can help in assessing whether the AI is acting morally. The authors highlight that AI pose a unique challenge in terms of moral accountability due to their potential to process vast amounts of information and make decisions that humans might not fully understand. They propose that XAI can help bridge the gap between AI and human moral accountability by making the AI's decision-making processes more transparent and understandable.

The paper concludes that while AI may eventually gain moral status, the current trajectory of AI development raises significant ethical concerns. The authors advocate for the development of XAI as a critical tool for ensuring that AI can be held accountable for their actions. They argue that XAI must meet certain epistemic goals, such as providing sufficient information about the AI's decision-making processes, to be effective in closing the moral accountability gap.

In summary, this paper emphasizes the importance of XAI in addressing the ethical challenges posed by AI, particularly in terms of moral accountability. By focusing on the epistemic access to AI decision-making processes, the authors provide a framework for developing AI systems that can be held accountable for their actions, thereby ensuring that AI can be integrated into society in a responsible and ethical manner.
