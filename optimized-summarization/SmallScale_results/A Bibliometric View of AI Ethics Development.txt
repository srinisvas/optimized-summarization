**Executive Summary**

The paper "A Bibliometric View of AI Ethics Development" explores the evolution of AI Ethics through a bibliometric analysis of literature from the last 20 years. The study identifies three distinct phases in the development of AI Ethics: an incubation phase, a phase where AI is made human-like, and a phase where AI is made human-centric. The authors argue that the next phase will likely focus on making AI more machine-like, as AI technology surpasses human capabilities.

The analysis reveals that AI Ethics has been a growing field since the 1980s, with significant developments in the 2010s. Key developments include the introduction of generative adversarial networks (GANs) in 2014, which have both excited and disappointed researchers. The paper highlights the importance of addressing ethical concerns related to AI, such as bias and opacity, as AI continues to advance. The authors conclude that AI Ethics must adapt to keep pace with technological advancements, emphasizing the need to prevent unethical AI development and ensure compliance with regulations like GDPR.

The work contributes to the ongoing discussions in AI Ethics by providing insights into the thematic focus of the literature. The study underscores the necessity of proactive ethical considerations to guide AI development and deployment, ensuring that AI remains a beneficial and responsible technology. Future research should focus on integrating ethical considerations into AI design and deployment strategies, particularly as AI technology continues to evolve and become more integrated into daily life.
