The research paper discusses the integration of ethical principles into AI systems to address data ownership, privacy, and systemic bias. It proposes a modular ethical assessment framework using ontological blocks, which are interpretable units encoding ethical principles such as fairness and accountability. The framework is designed to support transparent, accountable, and legally aligned AI evaluations, including compliance with the EU AI Act. The study demonstrates the effectiveness of the framework in real-world use cases, such as AI-powered investor profiling, by enabling dynamic, behavior-informed risk classification. However, challenges remain, including the manual effort required to build blocks and the reliance on probabilistic reasoning with incomplete data. The paper concludes that combining ontological blocks with FAIR principles offers a strong foundation for ethical AI monitoring, with key insights including the unified monitoring system's effectiveness in identifying ethical risks and supporting compliance, the structured and modular nature of ontological blocks, and the enhancement of discoverability, usability, and scalability through FAIR principles. The framework introduces ontological blocks as standardized, expert-designed tools for ethical AI assessment, with strengths in independence from training data and flexibility across domains, though challenges include automating block creation and managing dependencies on probabilistic models. Integrating ethical AI into legally binding decisions requires acknowledging varying legal standards and promoting transparency and cooperative oversight. The paper advocates for responsible AI development across sectors by addressing key challenges and providing a framework for ethical AI monitoring.
