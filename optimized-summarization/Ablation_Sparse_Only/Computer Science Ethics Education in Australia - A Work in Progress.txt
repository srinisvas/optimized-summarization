The research paper examines the ethical concerns related to AI and the preparation of undergraduates to navigate these issues. It finds that most Australian universities' Computer Science courses focus on micro-ethical concepts such as professionalism and compliance with professional and industry standards, neglecting macro-ethical agendas for the betterment of society and the planet. The study highlights the need for a more comprehensive understanding of how macro-ethics can be effectively incorporated into CS and IT education, addressing the gaps in current curricula and pedagogies. The project aims to survey the current status of ethics in Australian engineering universities, creating recommendations for content, teaching methods, and assessment of ethics learning, with a focus on integrating macro-ethical concepts. The research underscores the importance of ethics education in preparing graduates to understand the relationship between technology and society and actively promote sustainability, fairness, inclusivity, and social justice. It also emphasizes the need to translate ethical principles into policy and practice, acknowledging the ongoing challenges in this critical domain. The study suggests that there is a wide variation in the content of ethics courses in Australian CS curricula, with a greater emphasis on professional ethics and less on actively using technology to promote social good. The findings emphasize the need for a more comprehensive approach to ethics education, incorporating both micro-ethical concepts and macro-ethical agendas, to ensure that graduates are well-equipped to address the ethical implications of AI and other emerging technologies.
