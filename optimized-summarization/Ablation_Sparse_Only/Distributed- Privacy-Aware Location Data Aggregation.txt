he research paper introduces DIPALDA, an anonymization technique for distributed, privacy-aware location data aggregation on hierarchically structured personal location data. It addresses privacy concerns by using three privacy parameters: k-anonymity, minimum cloaking area size, and maximum re-identification probability. DIPALDA ensures that users can set individual privacy parameters to prevent re-identification and location privacy attacks. The technique is applied to various scenarios, including analyzing COVID-19 outbreaks and other sensitive data, and demonstrates a suitable trade-off between utility, privacy, and explainability. The paper emphasizes the importance of privacy parameters in building trust and promoting data sharing, as well as the need for explainability in privacy guarantees. DIPALDA can be adapted to different hierarchical structures, making it applicable to various use cases, such as analyzing employment status or sales activities in a company. The paper concludes by highlighting the significance of allowing data owners to control their privacy settings and the potential for DIPALDA to improve trust and acceptance of privacy approaches.
