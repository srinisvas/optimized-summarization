This research paper discusses the normative requirements for explainable AI (XAI) in the context of moral accountability. The authors argue that if developers of sophisticated AI fail to accommodate these requirements, humanity will miss the crucial moment when AI becomes accountable. They propose that XAI should meet at least three goals: making the relevant information available to humans, providing transparency in decision-making processes, and enabling the assessment of moral responsibility. The paper also touches on the challenges posed by AI's increasing complexity and inscrutability, as well as the potential for AI to gain moral status and the responsibilities that come with it. The authors suggest that Nomy Arpaly's account of moral-worthiness can be extended to AI and provide a model for how ethical theories can be used to issue normative requirements for AI explainability. The paper concludes by emphasizing the importance of AI explainability in closing the responsibility gap and addressing the ethical challenges posed by AI. The key phrases and important sentences highlight the central concerns and arguments of the paper, including the need for XAI to provide transparency, make relevant information available, and enable the assessment of moral responsibility. The paper bridges cross-disciplinary gaps and provides a framework for understanding the normative requirements for AI explainability in the context of moral accountability.
