The research paper introduces an adaptive differential privacy (ADP) mechanism for driver monitoring systems (DMS) that balances privacy, accuracy, and latency. The ADP method uses a combination of Laplace and Gaussian noise mechanisms to dynamically adjust privacy parameters based on the context and sensitivity of the data. The proposed mechanism has shown promising results with a 28.7% reduction in privacy leakage, +1.7% in utility (accuracy), and 10-20% shorter latencies compared to existing differential privacy methods. The ADP method is designed to be more robust against inference attacks and provides sufficient fidelity to justify collecting driver data for behavioral analytics, which is essential for policy enactments. Future work could explore integrating the ADP mechanism into federated learning frameworks and enhancing its adaptivity using machine learning models. Additionally, expanding the analysis to include adversarial robustness and energy efficiency would provide a more holistic assessment of privacy mechanisms in edge and mobile computing environments. The research also highlights the importance of privacy mechanisms in mobile crowd sensing and the need for secure offloading approaches to mitigate computational load. The proposed ADP mechanism represents a balanced approach between privacy, accuracy, and latency, making it suitable for practical use in deployment scenarios where both accuracy and speed are important. The ADP method is expected to have far better prospects in an edge-based computing space.
