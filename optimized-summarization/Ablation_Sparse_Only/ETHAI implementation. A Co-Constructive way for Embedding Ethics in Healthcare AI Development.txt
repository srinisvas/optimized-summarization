The ETHAI methodology is a multidisciplinary approach to integrating ethical values in AI systems for healthcare. It involves co-constructive processes with ethicists, technical developers, clinical experts, and potential users to define and operationalize ethical requirements. The methodology is characterized by four key principles: contextual awareness, practical application, cross-disciplinary collaboration, and iterative refinement. The implementation of ETHAI within the COMFORTage project has provided valuable insights into the challenges and benefits of integrating ethics into AI development for healthcare applications. The semi-structured approach has been effective in balancing normative frameworks with stakeholder engagement, and it has demonstrated the potential for meaningful ethical integration even when introduced at the design phase. Future work should focus on refining methods for operationalizing abstract ethical concepts, expanding participatory risk assessment, and developing more sophisticated tools for interdisciplinary communication. The ETHAI methodology contributes to the growing body of practical experience in ethical AI development, where concrete case studies remain scarce. The approach has shown advantages over conventional methods by engaging diverse stakeholders in collaborative requirements development, addressing cross-disciplinary communication barriers, and fostering greater appreciation of ethics considerations among technical partners. The methodology has been applied in a real-world healthcare AI development project, demonstrating its adaptability to complex contexts and the importance of early ethical integration. The findings inform the development of preventive strategies and patient stratification methods, and the approach represents a shift from traditional reactive care models to proactive care. The ETHAI methodology has the potential to improve the development of AI systems for healthcare applications by integrating ethics into the design phase, ensuring that ethical considerations are addressed from the outset. This approach can help identify domain-specific considerations, such as those related to dementia care and older adults' vulnerability, and can facilitate the development of effective risk mitigation strategies.
