The research paper explores the privacy concerns of young digital citizens, parents/educators, and AI professionals through the lens of Communication Privacy Management (CPM) Theory. It employs a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative data from structured questionnaires with qualitative insights from open-ended responses. The study identifies five key constructs: Data Ownership and Control (DOC), Parental Data Sharing (PDS), Perceived Risks and Benefits (PRB), Transparency and Trust (TT), and Education and Awareness (EA). The analysis reveals that Education and Awareness (EA) significantly influences Data Ownership and Control (DOC) and Perceived Risks and Benefits (PRB), highlighting the importance of privacy education in empowering young digital citizens. Transparency and Trust (TT) also positively influences Data Ownership and Control (DOC), underscoring the role of transparency in building trust and reducing privacy risks. However, Parental Data Sharing (PDS) has a negative impact on Data Ownership and Control (DOC), indicating the need for collaborative policymaking and user-centric design practices. The study emphasizes the need for integrating diverse stakeholders' perspectives in AI system development to address complex privacy challenges faced by young digital citizens. Recommendations include collaborative policymaking, user-centric design practices, and enhanced privacy education to empower stakeholders and reduce privacy risks. Future research should expand demographic diversity, refine constructs for improved reliability, and explore longitudinal shifts in privacy behaviors to further inform privacy-centric AI system designs. The research contributes to the application of CPM Theory in AI contexts, advancing the understanding of privacy management behaviors among young digital citizens and their stakeholders.
