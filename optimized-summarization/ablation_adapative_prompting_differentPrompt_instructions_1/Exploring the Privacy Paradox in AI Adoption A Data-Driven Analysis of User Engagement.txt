**Executive Summary**

This study examines the relationship between AI adoption and data privacy concerns, analyzing user behavior and engagement with AI-powered tools. Using a dataset of 656 participants, the research investigates how AI trust influences the use of chatbots, virtual assistants, and digital payment methods, as well as demographic factors such as age and country. The findings reveal a privacy paradox, where many users who distrust AI still engage with AI-powered tools, highlighting the need for greater transparency and user awareness. The study identifies key demographic groups, such as Millennials and Gen Z, as the most engaged with AI technologies, while Gen X and Baby Boomers show more skepticism. Regionally, Canada demonstrates the highest AI trust, while China and India show a balanced perspective. The analysis also shows no strong link between AI privacy concerns and digital payment use, suggesting that financial technology adoption is driven more by accessibility and digital behavior than trust in AI. The study advocates for stronger AI privacy policies, ethical data practices, and regulatory frameworks to ensure that AI development remains both innovative and privacy-conscious.
