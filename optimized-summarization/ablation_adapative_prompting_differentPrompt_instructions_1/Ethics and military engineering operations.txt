**Executive Summary**

This paper explores the intersection of military engineering ethics in warzones, focusing on construction engineering in combat environments. The author argues that while traditional engineering ethics principles can be applied, unique challenges arise in warzones, necessitating a nuanced approach. Key examples include the enforcement of safety standards and the prevention of corruption. The author proposes that in some cases, traditional engineering ethics principles may be inadequate, and just war theory considerations, particularly jus in bello and jus post bellum, could provide guidance. The paper calls for a reevaluation of engineering ethics principles in wartime contexts to address the specific dilemmas faced by military engineers in these environments.
